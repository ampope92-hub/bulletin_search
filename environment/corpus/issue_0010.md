Collections Care Digest, issue 10

## Item 1
Treatment of the delaminating veneer on the oak chest used a microcrystalline wax, applied by nebuliser in several passes. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Testing preceded each change of material. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal.

## Item 2
A brushed dammar varnish was applied to the plaster wall relief following removal of the earlier coating. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area.

## Item 3
A through crack in the bronze votive figure was rejoined with a pin of matching alloy. The join is visible on close inspection by design. Alignment was dry-fitted before any adhesive was introduced. The repair can be taken apart again if required.

## Item 4
Electron microscopy of a cross-section of the marble portrait bust identified bast fibre in the warp and protein fibre in the weft. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. The finding is consistent with the attributed date.

## Item 5
Cleaning removed greasy handling grime from the pewter tankard with swabs of a hydrocarbon and alcohol blend, working in one direction only. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water.

## Item 6
An infestation of webbing clothes moth in the wool tapestry was treated by localised heat treatment. No live activity was seen at the follow-up inspection. Trap counts have stayed at zero since. Debris was vacuumed away before treatment began.

## Item 7
Polarised light microscopy of the silk altar frontal identified an arsenic-bearing green in the border. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. The finding is consistent with the attributed date.

## Item 8
Radiography of the oak chest identified bast fibre in the warp and protein fibre in the weft. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Sampling was held to the minimum needed.

## Item 9
Cleaning removed adhesive residue from old tape from the silver caddy spoon with a pulsed laser at low fluence, working from the least sensitive area outward. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 10
Treatment of the flaking paint on the silver caddy spoon used a cellulose ether gel, applied under a heated spatula. Testing preceded each change of material. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 11
Infrared spectroscopy of the lacquer writing box identified a mercury-based red beneath a later overpaint. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. The finding is consistent with the attributed date.

## Item 12
Cleaning removed greasy handling grime from the glass roundel with a rigid chelating gel, working from the least sensitive area outward. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. A small window was left untouched for future reference.
