Studio Bulletin, issue 11

## Item 1
Electron microscopy of a cross-section of the vellum choir book identified a drying oil bound with a little pine resin. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. Sampling was held to the minimum needed.

## Item 2
Infrared spectroscopy of the silver caddy spoon identified bast fibre in the warp and protein fibre in the weft. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Results were checked against laboratory reference material.

## Item 3
The leather bookbinding was rehoused in a barrier-film enclosure with an oxygen scavenger, padded with unbuffered tissue. The fit was adjusted twice before it was accepted. The mount maker was consulted before the design was fixed. Handling is now possible without touching the surface.

## Item 4
Cleaning removed hard calcareous crusts from the leather bookbinding with grated vinyl crumb, working in short overlapping passes. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage.

## Item 5
Electron microscopy of a cross-section of the oak chest identified a drying oil bound with a little pine resin. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram.

## Item 6
The pewter tankard is displayed at fifty-five per cent relative humidity under a fifty lux limit. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes.

## Item 7
Treatment of the crizzled surface on the porcelain dish used a microcrystalline wax, applied through fine tissue. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Testing preceded each change of material.

## Item 8
Cleaning removed insect frass from the leather bookbinding with grated vinyl crumb, working from the least sensitive area outward. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 9
Cleaning removed a discoloured natural resin coating from the glass roundel with a cellulose poultice, working in short overlapping passes. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage.

## Item 10
A detached handle in the pewter tankard was rejoined with a reversible animal glue. Alignment was dry-fitted before any adhesive was introduced. The join is visible on close inspection by design. The repair can be taken apart again if required.

## Item 11
Cleaning removed insect frass from the papier-mache tea tray with grated vinyl crumb, working from the least sensitive area outward. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. A small window was left untouched for future reference.

## Item 12
The oak chest was rehoused in a carved inert foam cradle, padded with unbuffered tissue. Handling is now possible without touching the surface. The housing carries a printed handling instruction. The fit was adjusted twice before it was accepted.
