Conservation Notes, issue 14

## Item 1
Raman microscopy of the plaster wall relief identified a mercury-based red beneath a later overpaint. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. Results were checked against laboratory reference material.

## Item 2
The marble portrait bust is displayed at sixty per cent relative humidity under filtered daylight only. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter. The specification was agreed with the curator. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck.

## Item 3
Electron microscopy of a cross-section of the porcelain dish identified an arsenic-bearing green in the border. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. The finding is consistent with the attributed date.

## Item 4
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the leather bookbinding used a microcrystalline wax, applied by capillary feed along the edge. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 5
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the vellum choir book used a warm fish glue, applied under a heated spatula. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal.

## Item 6
A failed earlier repair in the ivory hair comb was rejoined with a reversible animal glue. The join is visible on close inspection by design. Alignment was dry-fitted before any adhesive was introduced. Clamping pressure was kept deliberately light.

## Item 7
A matte cellulose ether film was applied to the wool tapestry following consolidation of the powdering surface. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one.

## Item 8
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the linen sampler used a wheat starch paste, applied with a fine brush under magnification. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Testing preceded each change of material. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 9
A matte cellulose ether film was applied to the linen sampler following consolidation of the powdering surface. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area.

## Item 10
Electron microscopy of a cross-section of the leather bookbinding identified a mercury-based red beneath a later overpaint. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sampling was held to the minimum needed.

## Item 11
The vellum choir book is displayed at thirty-five per cent relative humidity under a two hundred lux limit with annual rotation. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. The specification was agreed with the curator.

## Item 12
The glass roundel was rehoused in an aluminium honeycomb panel, padded with unbuffered tissue. The mount maker was consulted before the design was fixed. The housing carries a printed handling instruction. The fit was adjusted twice before it was accepted.
