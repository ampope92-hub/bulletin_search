Conservation Notes, issue 17

## Item 1
A sprayed low-molecular-weight resin was applied to the glass roundel following toning of the fills with dry pigment. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure.

## Item 2
An old split along the grain in the oak chest was rejoined with hidden hardwood pegs. The join is visible on close inspection by design. The repair can be taken apart again if required. Clamping pressure was kept deliberately light.

## Item 3
Cleaning removed adhesive residue from old tape from the ivory hair comb with grated vinyl crumb, working in short overlapping passes. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. A small window was left untouched for future reference.

## Item 4
The pewter tankard is displayed at fifty-five per cent relative humidity under a fifty lux limit. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes.

## Item 5
Raman microscopy of the silk altar frontal identified an arsenic-bearing green in the border. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. The finding is consistent with the attributed date.

## Item 6
Cleaning removed greasy handling grime from the linen sampler with swabs of a hydrocarbon and alcohol blend, working from the least sensitive area outward. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water.

## Item 7
Cleaning removed greasy handling grime from the lacquer writing box with swabs of a hydrocarbon and alcohol blend, working with frequent renewal of the swab. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. A small window was left untouched for future reference.

## Item 8
Treatment of the flaking paint on the oak chest used a warm fish glue, applied by capillary feed along the edge. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Testing preceded each change of material. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 9
An old split along the grain in the lacquer writing box was rejoined with a shaped balsa fill. Clamping pressure was kept deliberately light. The repair can be taken apart again if required. Alignment was dry-fitted before any adhesive was introduced.

## Item 10
A detached handle in the pewter tankard was rejoined with a pin of matching alloy. The repair can be taken apart again if required. Clamping pressure was kept deliberately light. The join is visible on close inspection by design.

## Item 11
The pewter tankard is displayed at fifty-five per cent relative humidity under filtered daylight only. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes.

## Item 12
Cleaning removed adhesive residue from old tape from the marble portrait bust with a rigid chelating gel, working in short overlapping passes. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage.
