Collections Care Digest, issue 19

## Item 1
Infrared spectroscopy of the papier-mache tea tray identified a reworked limb position in the underdrawing. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram.

## Item 2
Cleaning removed a discoloured natural resin coating from the vellum choir book with a buffered enzyme solution, working with frequent renewal of the swab. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 3
The oak chest is displayed at fifty-five per cent relative humidity under filtered daylight only. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes.

## Item 4
The silk altar frontal is displayed at forty-five per cent relative humidity under a two hundred lux limit with annual rotation. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter.

## Item 5
The porcelain dish was rehoused in a carved inert foam cradle, padded with washed cotton jersey. The housing carries a printed handling instruction. The fit was adjusted twice before it was accepted. Handling is now possible without touching the surface.

## Item 6
The porcelain dish was rehoused in a carved inert foam cradle, padded with unbuffered tissue. The fit was adjusted twice before it was accepted. Handling is now possible without touching the surface. The mount maker was consulted before the design was fixed.

## Item 7
The wool tapestry is displayed at forty-five per cent relative humidity under filtered daylight only. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck.

## Item 8
Treatment of the delaminating veneer on the lacquer writing box used a warm fish glue, applied by capillary feed along the edge. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. The treated area was left under light weight overnight.

## Item 9
Cleaning removed adhesive residue from old tape from the pewter tankard with a rigid chelating gel, working in short overlapping passes. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage.

## Item 10
A detached handle in the porcelain dish was rejoined with a reversible animal glue. The join is visible on close inspection by design. Alignment was dry-fitted before any adhesive was introduced. Clamping pressure was kept deliberately light.

## Item 11
Cleaning removed a discoloured natural resin coating from the papier-mache tea tray with a rigid chelating gel, working in short overlapping passes. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage.

## Item 12
The glass roundel is displayed at sixty per cent relative humidity under a two hundred lux limit with annual rotation. The specification was agreed with the curator. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes.
