Collections Care Digest, issue 23

## Item 1
Cleaning removed sooty deposits from the oak chest with a rigid chelating gel, working from the least sensitive area outward. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage.

## Item 2
A matte cellulose ether film was applied to the porcelain dish following toning of the fills with dry pigment. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area. The finish was left to cure for a week before display.

## Item 3
Treatment of the flaking paint on the silver caddy spoon used a microcrystalline wax, applied by nebuliser in several passes. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Testing preceded each change of material.

## Item 4
Treatment of the cracked gilding on the papier-mache tea tray used a wheat starch paste, applied through fine tissue. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Testing preceded each change of material.

## Item 5
Cleaning removed sooty deposits from the wool tapestry with swabs of a hydrocarbon and alcohol blend, working in one direction only. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. A small window was left untouched for future reference.

## Item 6
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the vellum choir book used a seaweed-derived size, applied by capillary feed along the edge. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Testing preceded each change of material.

## Item 7
Raman microscopy of the oak chest identified bast fibre in the warp and protein fibre in the weft. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram.

## Item 8
The bronze votive figure is displayed at sixty per cent relative humidity under filtered daylight only. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck.

## Item 9
Treatment of the cupped and tented ground on the marble portrait bust used a cellulose ether gel, applied with a fine brush under magnification. Testing preceded each change of material. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal.

## Item 10
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the silk altar frontal used a cellulose ether gel, applied with a fine brush under magnification. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Testing preceded each change of material. The treated area was left under light weight overnight.

## Item 11
Cleaning removed sooty deposits from the leather bookbinding with a buffered enzyme solution, working with frequent renewal of the swab. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage.

## Item 12
Treatment of the crizzled surface on the glass roundel used a microcrystalline wax, applied under gentle vacuum suction. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Testing preceded each change of material.
