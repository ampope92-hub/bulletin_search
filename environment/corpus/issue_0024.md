Collections Care Digest, issue 24

## Item 1
A brushed dammar varnish was applied to the oak chest following toning of the fills with dry pigment. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure.

## Item 2
The ivory hair comb is displayed at fifty-five per cent relative humidity under a fifty lux limit. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes. The specification was agreed with the curator. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter.

## Item 3
Electron microscopy of a cross-section of the ivory hair comb identified bast fibre in the warp and protein fibre in the weft. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. Sampling was held to the minimum needed.

## Item 4
Treatment of the crizzled surface on the porcelain dish used a microcrystalline wax, applied under gentle vacuum suction. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. The treated area was left under light weight overnight.

## Item 5
A buffed microcrystalline wax was applied to the lacquer writing box following toning of the fills with dry pigment. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one.

## Item 6
Cleaning removed adhesive residue from old tape from the silk altar frontal with a rigid chelating gel, working with frequent renewal of the swab. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 7
Electron microscopy of a cross-section of the glass roundel identified a reworked limb position in the underdrawing. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram.

## Item 8
Raman microscopy of the papier-mache tea tray identified a drying oil bound with a little pine resin. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. Results were checked against laboratory reference material.
