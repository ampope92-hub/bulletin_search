Conservation Notes, issue 26

## Item 1
A failed earlier repair in the wool tapestry was rejoined with a reversible animal glue. Clamping pressure was kept deliberately light. Alignment was dry-fitted before any adhesive was introduced. The join is visible on close inspection by design.

## Item 2
A detached handle in the pewter tankard was rejoined with a reversible animal glue. Alignment was dry-fitted before any adhesive was introduced. The join is visible on close inspection by design. The repair can be taken apart again if required.

## Item 3
Polarised light microscopy of the porcelain dish identified a double ground of chalk under lead white. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Sampling was held to the minimum needed.

## Item 4
The plaster wall relief is displayed at fifty-five per cent relative humidity under a two hundred lux limit with annual rotation. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck.

## Item 5
Treatment of the flaking paint on the silver caddy spoon used a dilute acrylic resin, applied under a heated spatula. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Testing preceded each change of material. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 6
The glass roundel is displayed at forty-five per cent relative humidity under a fifty lux limit. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes. The specification was agreed with the curator.

## Item 7
A failed earlier repair in the linen sampler was rejoined with a reversible animal glue. The join is visible on close inspection by design. Alignment was dry-fitted before any adhesive was introduced. The repair can be taken apart again if required.

## Item 8
Cleaning removed hard calcareous crusts from the wool tapestry with a buffered enzyme solution, working from the least sensitive area outward. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 9
A brushed dammar varnish was applied to the marble portrait bust following toning of the fills with dry pigment. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one.

## Item 10
Cleaning removed adhesive residue from old tape from the pewter tankard with a pulsed laser at low fluence, working from the least sensitive area outward. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water.

## Item 11
A failed earlier repair in the bronze votive figure was rejoined with a reversible animal glue. Clamping pressure was kept deliberately light. The join is visible on close inspection by design. Alignment was dry-fitted before any adhesive was introduced.

## Item 12
A matte cellulose ether film was applied to the ivory hair comb following consolidation of the powdering surface. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure. The finish was left to cure for a week before display.
