Conservation Notes, issue 30

## Item 1
Treatment of the flaking paint on the bronze votive figure used a microcrystalline wax, applied by capillary feed along the edge. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Testing preceded each change of material. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 2
A matte cellulose ether film was applied to the glass roundel following toning of the fills with dry pigment. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure. The finish was left to cure for a week before display.

## Item 3
A buffed microcrystalline wax was applied to the plaster wall relief following consolidation of the powdering surface. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one.

## Item 4
The marble portrait bust was rehoused in an aluminium honeycomb panel, padded with unbuffered tissue. Handling is now possible without touching the surface. The fit was adjusted twice before it was accepted. The mount maker was consulted before the design was fixed.

## Item 5
Radiography of the pewter tankard identified an arsenic-bearing green in the border. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. The finding is consistent with the attributed date.

## Item 6
Treatment of the blistered varnish on the ivory hair comb used a wheat starch paste, applied by nebuliser in several passes. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Testing preceded each change of material.

## Item 7
The vellum choir book was rehoused in a drop-back clamshell box, padded with washed cotton jersey. The housing carries a printed handling instruction. Handling is now possible without touching the surface. The fit was adjusted twice before it was accepted.

## Item 8
A sprayed low-molecular-weight resin was applied to the wool tapestry following toning of the fills with dry pigment. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area.

## Item 9
Cleaning removed sooty deposits from the wool tapestry with swabs of a hydrocarbon and alcohol blend, working with frequent renewal of the swab. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage.

## Item 10
The marble portrait bust is displayed at sixty per cent relative humidity under a hundred and fifty lux limit. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter. The specification was agreed with the curator.

## Item 11
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the silk altar frontal used a dilute acrylic resin, applied through fine tissue. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Testing preceded each change of material. The treated area was left under light weight overnight.

## Item 12
Cleaning removed hard calcareous crusts from the glass roundel with a rigid chelating gel, working in short overlapping passes. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.
