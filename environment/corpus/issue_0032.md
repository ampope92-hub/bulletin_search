Collections Care Digest, issue 32

## Item 1
The papier-mache tea tray was rehoused in a carved inert foam cradle, padded with unbuffered tissue. Handling is now possible without touching the surface. The fit was adjusted twice before it was accepted. The mount maker was consulted before the design was fixed.

## Item 2
Treatment of the flaking paint on the oak chest used a microcrystalline wax, applied under a heated spatula. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Testing preceded each change of material.

## Item 3
A failed earlier repair in the vellum choir book was rejoined with a reversible animal glue. Clamping pressure was kept deliberately light. The repair can be taken apart again if required. The join is visible on close inspection by design.

## Item 4
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the linen sampler used a warm fish glue, applied with a fine brush under magnification. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 5
Cleaning removed a discoloured natural resin coating from the ivory hair comb with a rigid chelating gel, working with frequent renewal of the swab. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. A small window was left untouched for future reference.

## Item 6
Polarised light microscopy of the wool tapestry identified bast fibre in the warp and protein fibre in the weft. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram.

## Item 7
Treatment of the cupped and tented ground on the lacquer writing box used a dilute acrylic resin, applied by capillary feed along the edge. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 8
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the linen sampler used an isinglass solution, applied under gentle vacuum suction. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 9
The silver caddy spoon is displayed at forty-five per cent relative humidity under a two hundred lux limit with annual rotation. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck.

## Item 10
A buffed microcrystalline wax was applied to the pewter tankard following corrosion inhibition with an azole. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure.

## Item 11
The porcelain dish is displayed at forty-five per cent relative humidity under a fifty lux limit. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. The specification was agreed with the curator. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes.

## Item 12
Cleaning removed adhesive residue from old tape from the oak chest with a rigid chelating gel, working from the least sensitive area outward. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. A small window was left untouched for future reference.
