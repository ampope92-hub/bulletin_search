Studio Bulletin, issue 33

## Item 1
Cleaning removed a discoloured natural resin coating from the bronze votive figure with a pulsed laser at low fluence, working in one direction only. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. A small window was left untouched for future reference.

## Item 2
A buffed microcrystalline wax was applied to the bronze votive figure following removal of the earlier coating. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area. The finish was left to cure for a week before display.

## Item 3
Treatment of the flaking paint on the papier-mache tea tray used a seaweed-derived size, applied by capillary feed along the edge. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Testing preceded each change of material. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 4
Radiography of the wool tapestry identified an arsenic-bearing green in the border. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. Sampling was held to the minimum needed.

## Item 5
Cleaning removed hard calcareous crusts from the silk altar frontal with a buffered enzyme solution, working with frequent renewal of the swab. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage.

## Item 6
A sprayed low-molecular-weight resin was applied to the bronze votive figure following toning of the fills with dry pigment. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one. The finish was left to cure for a week before display.

## Item 7
The pewter tankard is displayed at sixty per cent relative humidity under a fifty lux limit. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. The specification was agreed with the curator. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes.

## Item 8
An infestation of webbing clothes moth in the linen sampler was treated by a fortnight in a low-oxygen atmosphere. Trap counts have stayed at zero since. No live activity was seen at the follow-up inspection. Debris was vacuumed away before treatment began.

## Item 9
Treatment of the flaking paint on the pewter tankard used a cellulose ether gel, applied through fine tissue. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Testing preceded each change of material. The treated area was left under light weight overnight.

## Item 10
Radiography of the lacquer writing box identified a drying oil bound with a little pine resin. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. The finding is consistent with the attributed date.

## Item 11
Cleaning removed greasy handling grime from the ivory hair comb with a rigid chelating gel, working from the least sensitive area outward. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 12
X-ray fluorescence of the wool tapestry identified an arsenic-bearing green in the border. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. Sampling was held to the minimum needed.
