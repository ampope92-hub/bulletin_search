Collections Care Digest, issue 40

## Item 1
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the linen sampler used a microcrystalline wax, applied under gentle vacuum suction. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Testing preceded each change of material. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal.

## Item 2
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the linen sampler used an isinglass solution, applied under a heated spatula. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Testing preceded each change of material.

## Item 3
Cleaning removed adhesive residue from old tape from the pewter tankard with a rigid chelating gel, working with frequent renewal of the swab. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 4
Cleaning removed sooty deposits from the leather bookbinding with a buffered enzyme solution, working with frequent renewal of the swab. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage.

## Item 5
Electron microscopy of a cross-section of the glass roundel identified a double ground of chalk under lead white. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sampling was held to the minimum needed.

## Item 6
A brushed dammar varnish was applied to the papier-mache tea tray following toning of the fills with dry pigment. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure.

## Item 7
Cleaning removed greasy handling grime from the wool tapestry with a rigid chelating gel, working in short overlapping passes. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage.

## Item 8
Raman microscopy of the pewter tankard identified a double ground of chalk under lead white. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. Results were checked against laboratory reference material.

## Item 9
The marble portrait bust is displayed at forty-five per cent relative humidity under filtered daylight only. The specification was agreed with the curator. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck.

## Item 10
Cleaning removed adhesive residue from old tape from the oak chest with a rigid chelating gel, working in short overlapping passes. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water.

## Item 11
The silk altar frontal is displayed at fifty-five per cent relative humidity under a hundred and fifty lux limit. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes. The specification was agreed with the curator.

## Item 12
Raman microscopy of the ivory hair comb identified a double ground of chalk under lead white. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sampling was held to the minimum needed.
