Conservation Notes, issue 42

## Item 1
Treatment of the cracked gilding on the papier-mache tea tray used a wheat starch paste, applied by capillary feed along the edge. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Testing preceded each change of material.

## Item 2
Treatment of the cupped and tented ground on the marble portrait bust used a microcrystalline wax, applied by nebuliser in several passes. Testing preceded each change of material. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. The treated area was left under light weight overnight.

## Item 3
The oak chest is displayed at fifty-five per cent relative humidity under a two hundred lux limit with annual rotation. The specification was agreed with the curator. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck.

## Item 4
Cleaning removed hard calcareous crusts from the silver caddy spoon with a pulsed laser at low fluence, working in short overlapping passes. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water.

## Item 5
Electron microscopy of a cross-section of the vellum choir book identified a drying oil bound with a little pine resin. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. Sampling was held to the minimum needed.

## Item 6
Cleaning removed a discoloured natural resin coating from the silver caddy spoon with a rigid chelating gel, working from the least sensitive area outward. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water.

## Item 7
An infestation of webbing clothes moth in the wool tapestry was treated by controlled freezing with staged warming. Trap counts have stayed at zero since. Neighbouring storage was surveyed at the same time. Debris was vacuumed away before treatment began.

## Item 8
Treatment of the crizzled surface on the porcelain dish used a microcrystalline wax, applied under gentle vacuum suction. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. The treated area was left under light weight overnight.

## Item 9
Cleaning removed insect frass from the papier-mache tea tray with grated vinyl crumb, working with frequent renewal of the swab. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. A small window was left untouched for future reference.

## Item 10
Raman microscopy of the vellum choir book identified a double ground of chalk under lead white. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. The finding is consistent with the attributed date.

## Item 11
Treatment of the powdering pigment on the ivory hair comb used a wheat starch paste, applied with a fine brush under magnification. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal.

## Item 12
The pewter tankard was rehoused in a carved inert foam cradle, padded with unbuffered tissue. Handling is now possible without touching the surface. The housing carries a printed handling instruction. The mount maker was consulted before the design was fixed.
