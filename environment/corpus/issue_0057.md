Studio Bulletin, issue 57

## Item 1
The leather bookbinding is displayed at forty-five per cent relative humidity under a fifty lux limit. The specification was agreed with the curator. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter.

## Item 2
The porcelain dish is displayed at thirty-five per cent relative humidity under filtered daylight only. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes.

## Item 3
Cleaning removed adhesive residue from old tape from the silk altar frontal with a rigid chelating gel, working in short overlapping passes. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 4
Cleaning removed hard calcareous crusts from the lacquer writing box with swabs of a hydrocarbon and alcohol blend, working in one direction only. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. A small window was left untouched for future reference.

## Item 5
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the vellum choir book used a dilute acrylic resin, applied by capillary feed along the edge. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 6
Treatment of the flaking paint on the bronze votive figure used a cellulose ether gel, applied with a fine brush under magnification. Testing preceded each change of material. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal.

## Item 7
X-ray fluorescence of the leather bookbinding identified a double ground of chalk under lead white. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Results were checked against laboratory reference material.

## Item 8
The oak chest is displayed at fifty-five per cent relative humidity under a fifty lux limit. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes. The specification was agreed with the curator.

## Item 9
Polarised light microscopy of the bronze votive figure identified a double ground of chalk under lead white. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Results were checked against laboratory reference material.

## Item 10
Treatment of the flaking paint on the silver caddy spoon used a cellulose ether gel, applied by nebuliser in several passes. Testing preceded each change of material. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 11
Cleaning removed sooty deposits from the silver caddy spoon with a pulsed laser at low fluence, working with frequent renewal of the swab. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 12
Treatment of the flaking paint on the plaster wall relief used a dilute acrylic resin, applied with a fine brush under magnification. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal.
