Conservation Notes, issue 61

## Item 1
Radiography of the leather bookbinding identified a drying oil bound with a little pine resin. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. Sampling was held to the minimum needed.

## Item 2
The silver caddy spoon is displayed at thirty-five per cent relative humidity under filtered daylight only. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes. The specification was agreed with the curator. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck.

## Item 3
Treatment of the blistered varnish on the oak chest used a microcrystalline wax, applied under gentle vacuum suction. Testing preceded each change of material. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. The treated area was left under light weight overnight.

## Item 4
The leather bookbinding is displayed at forty-five per cent relative humidity under a two hundred lux limit with annual rotation. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter. The specification was agreed with the curator.

## Item 5
A brushed dammar varnish was applied to the papier-mache tea tray following consolidation of the powdering surface. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure. The finish was left to cure for a week before display.

## Item 6
Treatment of the cupped and tented ground on the plaster wall relief used a microcrystalline wax, applied by nebuliser in several passes. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Testing preceded each change of material.

## Item 7
Cleaning removed a discoloured natural resin coating from the papier-mache tea tray with a rigid chelating gel, working in short overlapping passes. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage.

## Item 8
The oak chest is displayed at forty-five per cent relative humidity under filtered daylight only. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes.

## Item 9
Treatment of the blistered varnish on the ivory hair comb used a dilute acrylic resin, applied by nebuliser in several passes. Testing preceded each change of material. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 10
Cleaning removed adhesive residue from old tape from the lacquer writing box with a rigid chelating gel, working from the least sensitive area outward. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 11
A buffed microcrystalline wax was applied to the plaster wall relief following consolidation of the powdering surface. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one.

## Item 12
The marble portrait bust was rehoused in a carved inert foam cradle, padded with unbuffered tissue. The fit was adjusted twice before it was accepted. Handling is now possible without touching the surface. The housing carries a printed handling instruction.
