Studio Bulletin, issue 64

## Item 1
Cleaning removed a discoloured natural resin coating from the silk altar frontal with a rigid chelating gel, working in one direction only. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage.

## Item 2
Treatment of the powdering pigment on the ivory hair comb used a wheat starch paste, applied with a fine brush under magnification. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal.

## Item 3
Radiography of the lacquer writing box identified a drying oil bound with a little pine resin. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. The finding is consistent with the attributed date.

## Item 4
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the silk altar frontal used a seaweed-derived size, applied through fine tissue. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Testing preceded each change of material.

## Item 5
The vellum choir book was rehoused in a carved inert foam cradle, padded with unbuffered tissue. Handling is now possible without touching the surface. The mount maker was consulted before the design was fixed. The fit was adjusted twice before it was accepted.

## Item 6
The silk altar frontal is displayed at forty-five per cent relative humidity under a fifty lux limit. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes. The specification was agreed with the curator. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter.

## Item 7
Treatment of the blistered varnish on the papier-mache tea tray used an isinglass solution, applied by nebuliser in several passes. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 8
A buffed microcrystalline wax was applied to the lacquer writing box following consolidation of the powdering surface. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure.

## Item 9
Cleaning removed hard calcareous crusts from the glass roundel with a pulsed laser at low fluence, working with frequent renewal of the swab. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. A small window was left untouched for future reference.

## Item 10
The silk altar frontal is displayed at fifty-five per cent relative humidity under a two hundred lux limit with annual rotation. The specification was agreed with the curator. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter.

## Item 11
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the linen sampler used a dilute acrylic resin, applied through fine tissue. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Testing preceded each change of material.

## Item 12
Raman microscopy of the papier-mache tea tray identified a reworked limb position in the underdrawing. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. The finding is consistent with the attributed date.
