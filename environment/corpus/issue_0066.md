Studio Bulletin, issue 66

## Item 1
Treatment of the blistered varnish on the lacquer writing box used a microcrystalline wax, applied by nebuliser in several passes. Testing preceded each change of material. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 2
Cleaning removed hard calcareous crusts from the glass roundel with swabs of a hydrocarbon and alcohol blend, working with frequent renewal of the swab. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage.

## Item 3
Cleaning removed a discoloured natural resin coating from the silk altar frontal with a rigid chelating gel, working in one direction only. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage.

## Item 4
Cleaning removed a discoloured natural resin coating from the lacquer writing box with a rigid chelating gel, working with frequent renewal of the swab. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water.

## Item 5
Treatment of the flaking paint on the plaster wall relief used a microcrystalline wax, applied under a heated spatula. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Testing preceded each change of material.

## Item 6
Cleaning removed a discoloured natural resin coating from the glass roundel with swabs of a hydrocarbon and alcohol blend, working in short overlapping passes. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water.

## Item 7
Treatment of the flaking paint on the pewter tankard used a cellulose ether gel, applied with a fine brush under magnification. Testing preceded each change of material. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. The treated area was left under light weight overnight.

## Item 8
Radiography of the lacquer writing box identified an arsenic-bearing green in the border. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram.

## Item 9
Radiography of the lacquer writing box identified a mercury-based red beneath a later overpaint. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram.

## Item 10
A sprayed low-molecular-weight resin was applied to the silk altar frontal following toning of the fills with dry pigment. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure. The finish was left to cure for a week before display.

## Item 11
The porcelain dish is displayed at thirty-five per cent relative humidity under a hundred and fifty lux limit. The specification was agreed with the curator. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck.

## Item 12
An old split along the grain in the oak chest was rejoined with hidden hardwood pegs. The join is visible on close inspection by design. The repair can be taken apart again if required. Clamping pressure was kept deliberately light.
