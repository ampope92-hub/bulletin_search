Conservation Notes, issue 68

## Item 1
The marble portrait bust was rehoused in an aluminium honeycomb panel, padded with washed cotton jersey. Handling is now possible without touching the surface. The fit was adjusted twice before it was accepted. The mount maker was consulted before the design was fixed.

## Item 2
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the wool tapestry used a microcrystalline wax, applied by nebuliser in several passes. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Testing preceded each change of material.

## Item 3
Infrared spectroscopy of the plaster wall relief identified a mercury-based red beneath a later overpaint. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram.

## Item 4
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the silk altar frontal used a microcrystalline wax, applied by nebuliser in several passes. Testing preceded each change of material. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal.

## Item 5
The marble portrait bust was rehoused in a carved inert foam cradle, padded with washed cotton jersey. The mount maker was consulted before the design was fixed. The fit was adjusted twice before it was accepted. Handling is now possible without touching the surface.

## Item 6
X-ray fluorescence of the linen sampler identified bast fibre in the warp and protein fibre in the weft. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. The finding is consistent with the attributed date.

## Item 7
A failed earlier repair in the marble portrait bust was rejoined with a reversible animal glue. Alignment was dry-fitted before any adhesive was introduced. The repair can be taken apart again if required. The join is visible on close inspection by design.

## Item 8
Cleaning removed adhesive residue from old tape from the plaster wall relief with a cellulose poultice, working in one direction only. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. A small window was left untouched for future reference.

## Item 9
Cleaning removed a discoloured natural resin coating from the leather bookbinding with a buffered enzyme solution, working in one direction only. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage.

## Item 10
Raman microscopy of the wool tapestry identified a double ground of chalk under lead white. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram.

## Item 11
A matte cellulose ether film was applied to the lacquer writing box following toning of the fills with dry pigment. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure. The finish was left to cure for a week before display.

## Item 12
Treatment of the powdering pigment on the marble portrait bust used a lime nanoparticle dispersion, applied by capillary feed along the edge. Testing preceded each change of material. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.
