Studio Bulletin, issue 77

## Item 1
Treatment of the powdering pigment on the ivory hair comb used a warm fish glue, applied with a fine brush under magnification. Testing preceded each change of material. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. The treated area was left under light weight overnight.

## Item 2
A buffed microcrystalline wax was applied to the pewter tankard following consolidation of the powdering surface. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure.

## Item 3
Raman microscopy of the wool tapestry identified bast fibre in the warp and protein fibre in the weft. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sampling was held to the minimum needed.

## Item 4
The wool tapestry is displayed at fifty-five per cent relative humidity under a two hundred lux limit with annual rotation. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter.

## Item 5
Radiography of the glass roundel identified an arsenic-bearing green in the border. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. The finding is consistent with the attributed date.

## Item 6
The papier-mache tea tray was rehoused in a carved inert foam cradle, padded with washed cotton jersey. Handling is now possible without touching the surface. The housing carries a printed handling instruction. The fit was adjusted twice before it was accepted.

## Item 7
A failed earlier repair in the wool tapestry was rejoined with thread-by-thread bridging. The join is visible on close inspection by design. Alignment was dry-fitted before any adhesive was introduced. The repair can be taken apart again if required.

## Item 8
Cleaning removed greasy handling grime from the papier-mache tea tray with a rigid chelating gel, working with frequent renewal of the swab. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage.

## Item 9
Cleaning removed sooty deposits from the papier-mache tea tray with grated vinyl crumb, working in one direction only. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water.

## Item 10
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the vellum choir book used a microcrystalline wax, applied by capillary feed along the edge. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Testing preceded each change of material. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal.

## Item 11
A matte cellulose ether film was applied to the bronze votive figure following removal of the earlier coating. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one.

## Item 12
Polarised light microscopy of the glass roundel identified a drying oil bound with a little pine resin. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram.
