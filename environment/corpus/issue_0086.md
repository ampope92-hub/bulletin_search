Conservation Notes, issue 86

## Item 1
Cleaning removed adhesive residue from old tape from the vellum choir book with swabs of a hydrocarbon and alcohol blend, working with frequent renewal of the swab. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water.

## Item 2
A matte cellulose ether film was applied to the vellum choir book following toning of the fills with dry pigment. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one.

## Item 3
Electron microscopy of a cross-section of the bronze votive figure identified bast fibre in the warp and protein fibre in the weft. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sampling was held to the minimum needed.

## Item 4
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the vellum choir book used an isinglass solution, applied with a fine brush under magnification. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. The treated area was left under light weight overnight.

## Item 5
The silk altar frontal was rehoused in a coated brass tension strap, padded with unbuffered tissue. Handling is now possible without touching the surface. The mount maker was consulted before the design was fixed. The housing carries a printed handling instruction.

## Item 6
Raman microscopy of the lacquer writing box identified an arsenic-bearing green in the border. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. The finding is consistent with the attributed date.

## Item 7
Cleaning removed greasy handling grime from the bronze votive figure with swabs of a hydrocarbon and alcohol blend, working from the least sensitive area outward. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 8
X-ray fluorescence of the wool tapestry identified bast fibre in the warp and protein fibre in the weft. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sampling was held to the minimum needed.

## Item 9
Treatment of the flaking paint on the silver caddy spoon used a cellulose ether gel, applied with a fine brush under magnification. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Testing preceded each change of material. The treated area was left under light weight overnight.

## Item 10
X-ray fluorescence of the pewter tankard identified a mercury-based red beneath a later overpaint. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. Sampling was held to the minimum needed.

## Item 11
Treatment of the cracked gilding on the lacquer writing box used a dilute acrylic resin, applied under gentle vacuum suction. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Testing preceded each change of material.

## Item 12
A sprayed low-molecular-weight resin was applied to the porcelain dish following removal of the earlier coating. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area. The finish was left to cure for a week before display.
