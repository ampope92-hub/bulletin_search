Collections Care Digest, issue 92

## Item 1
Radiography of the wool tapestry identified a drying oil bound with a little pine resin. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. Sampling was held to the minimum needed.

## Item 2
Cleaning removed adhesive residue from old tape from the papier-mache tea tray with swabs of a hydrocarbon and alcohol blend, working from the least sensitive area outward. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 3
Treatment of the flaking paint on the ivory hair comb used a microcrystalline wax, applied under a heated spatula. Testing preceded each change of material. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal.

## Item 4
Cleaning removed greasy handling grime from the linen sampler with a buffered enzyme solution, working in one direction only. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water.

## Item 5
An infestation of carpet beetle larvae in the wool tapestry was treated by controlled freezing with staged warming. Trap counts have stayed at zero since. No live activity was seen at the follow-up inspection. Neighbouring storage was surveyed at the same time.

## Item 6
Cleaning removed hard calcareous crusts from the glass roundel with a cellulose poultice, working with frequent renewal of the swab. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 7
A sprayed low-molecular-weight resin was applied to the wool tapestry following toning of the fills with dry pigment. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area.

## Item 8
The linen sampler is displayed at forty-five per cent relative humidity under filtered daylight only. The specification was agreed with the curator. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter.

## Item 9
The leather bookbinding is displayed at sixty per cent relative humidity under a fifty lux limit. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes. The specification was agreed with the curator.

## Item 10
Infrared spectroscopy of the vellum choir book identified a drying oil bound with a little pine resin. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. The finding is consistent with the attributed date.

## Item 11
Treatment of the flaking paint on the marble portrait bust used a microcrystalline wax, applied through fine tissue. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal.

## Item 12
The silk altar frontal was rehoused in a carved inert foam cradle, padded with polyester wadding. The mount maker was consulted before the design was fixed. The housing carries a printed handling instruction. Handling is now possible without touching the surface.
