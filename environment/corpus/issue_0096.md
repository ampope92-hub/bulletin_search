Studio Bulletin, issue 96

## Item 1
Treatment of the cracked gilding on the oak chest used a cellulose ether gel, applied under a heated spatula. Testing preceded each change of material. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. The treated area was left under light weight overnight.

## Item 2
Treatment of the powdering pigment on the plaster wall relief used a dilute acrylic resin, applied under a heated spatula. Testing preceded each change of material. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 3
The glass roundel was rehoused in an aluminium honeycomb panel, padded with washed cotton jersey. The housing carries a printed handling instruction. The fit was adjusted twice before it was accepted. The mount maker was consulted before the design was fixed.

## Item 4
The glass roundel is displayed at thirty-five per cent relative humidity under filtered daylight only. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes. The specification was agreed with the curator.

## Item 5
The silver caddy spoon is displayed at thirty-five per cent relative humidity under filtered daylight only. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes. The specification was agreed with the curator. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck.

## Item 6
Treatment of the flaking paint on the papier-mache tea tray used a seaweed-derived size, applied with a fine brush under magnification. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Testing preceded each change of material.

## Item 7
Cleaning removed greasy handling grime from the plaster wall relief with grated vinyl crumb, working in one direction only. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. A small window was left untouched for future reference.

## Item 8
A matte cellulose ether film was applied to the oak chest following consolidation of the powdering surface. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one. The finish was left to cure for a week before display.

## Item 9
Cleaning removed insect frass from the lacquer writing box with a rigid chelating gel, working in one direction only. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water.

## Item 10
Cleaning removed greasy handling grime from the leather bookbinding with a rigid chelating gel, working in one direction only. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage.

## Item 11
Cleaning removed adhesive residue from old tape from the marble portrait bust with a rigid chelating gel, working in short overlapping passes. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage.

## Item 12
Cleaning removed a discoloured natural resin coating from the silver caddy spoon with a pulsed laser at low fluence, working in one direction only. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.
