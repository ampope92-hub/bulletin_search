Studio Bulletin, issue 101

## Item 1
Cleaning removed a discoloured natural resin coating from the glass roundel with a pulsed laser at low fluence, working in short overlapping passes. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage.

## Item 2
A sprayed low-molecular-weight resin was applied to the pewter tankard following corrosion inhibition with an azole. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area.

## Item 3
Cleaning removed sooty deposits from the bronze votive figure with a pulsed laser at low fluence, working from the least sensitive area outward. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage.

## Item 4
A sprayed low-molecular-weight resin was applied to the papier-mache tea tray following consolidation of the powdering surface. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure.

## Item 5
A shattered rim in the pewter tankard was rejoined with a pin of matching alloy. The join is visible on close inspection by design. The repair can be taken apart again if required. Alignment was dry-fitted before any adhesive was introduced.

## Item 6
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the wool tapestry used a cellulose ether gel, applied by nebuliser in several passes. Testing preceded each change of material. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 7
The lacquer writing box is displayed at thirty-five per cent relative humidity under filtered daylight only. The specification was agreed with the curator. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes.

## Item 8
The papier-mache tea tray is displayed at thirty-five per cent relative humidity under a two hundred lux limit with annual rotation. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. The specification was agreed with the curator. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter.

## Item 9
Cleaning removed a discoloured natural resin coating from the silver caddy spoon with swabs of a hydrocarbon and alcohol blend, working with frequent renewal of the swab. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 10
A matte cellulose ether film was applied to the ivory hair comb following consolidation of the powdering surface. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure. The finish was left to cure for a week before display.

## Item 11
Treatment of the blistered varnish on the ivory hair comb used an isinglass solution, applied by nebuliser in several passes. Testing preceded each change of material. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 12
An infestation of silverfish in the leather bookbinding was treated by isolation and monitoring with blunder traps. No live activity was seen at the follow-up inspection. Trap counts have stayed at zero since. Debris was vacuumed away before treatment began.
