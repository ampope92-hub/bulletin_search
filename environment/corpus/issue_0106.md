Collections Care Digest, issue 106

## Item 1
Cleaning removed sooty deposits from the glass roundel with a cellulose poultice, working with frequent renewal of the swab. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. A small window was left untouched for future reference.

## Item 2
Treatment of the cupped and tented ground on the lacquer writing box used a dilute acrylic resin, applied through fine tissue. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Testing preceded each change of material.

## Item 3
Treatment of the powdering pigment on the marble portrait bust used a lime nanoparticle dispersion, applied under gentle vacuum suction. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Testing preceded each change of material.

## Item 4
Cleaning removed insect frass from the papier-mache tea tray with a rigid chelating gel, working in one direction only. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water.

## Item 5
The leather bookbinding is displayed at sixty per cent relative humidity under a hundred and fifty lux limit. The specification was agreed with the curator. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter.

## Item 6
Treatment of the flaking paint on the bronze votive figure used a microcrystalline wax, applied through fine tissue. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 7
The lacquer writing box is displayed at fifty-five per cent relative humidity under a two hundred lux limit with annual rotation. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes. The specification was agreed with the curator. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck.

## Item 8
A buffed microcrystalline wax was applied to the lacquer writing box following toning of the fills with dry pigment. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one.

## Item 9
Raman microscopy of the silver caddy spoon identified a drying oil bound with a little pine resin. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. The finding is consistent with the attributed date.

## Item 10
A buffed microcrystalline wax was applied to the bronze votive figure following removal of the earlier coating. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area. The finish was left to cure for a week before display.

## Item 11
An infestation of webbing clothes moth in the oak chest was treated by isolation and monitoring with blunder traps. Trap counts have stayed at zero since. Neighbouring storage was surveyed at the same time. Debris was vacuumed away before treatment began.

## Item 12
The wool tapestry was rehoused in a carved inert foam cradle, padded with silk crepeline. The fit was adjusted twice before it was accepted. Handling is now possible without touching the surface. The mount maker was consulted before the design was fixed.
