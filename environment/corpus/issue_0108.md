Studio Bulletin, issue 108

## Item 1
Cleaning removed adhesive residue from old tape from the glass roundel with a cellulose poultice, working from the least sensitive area outward. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. A small window was left untouched for future reference.

## Item 2
A brushed dammar varnish was applied to the marble portrait bust following toning of the fills with dry pigment. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one.

## Item 3
Cleaning removed adhesive residue from old tape from the plaster wall relief with a pulsed laser at low fluence, working in short overlapping passes. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. A small window was left untouched for future reference.

## Item 4
Treatment of the powdering pigment on the plaster wall relief used a microcrystalline wax, applied with a fine brush under magnification. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 5
Treatment of the blistered varnish on the ivory hair comb used a wheat starch paste, applied by nebuliser in several passes. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Testing preceded each change of material.

## Item 6
The oak chest is displayed at fifty-five per cent relative humidity under filtered daylight only. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes.

## Item 7
Electron microscopy of a cross-section of the vellum choir book identified a reworked limb position in the underdrawing. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Results were checked against laboratory reference material.

## Item 8
A through crack in the silver caddy spoon was rejoined with a pin of matching alloy. The repair can be taken apart again if required. Clamping pressure was kept deliberately light. The join is visible on close inspection by design.

## Item 9
A detached handle in the silver caddy spoon was rejoined with a pin of matching alloy. The repair can be taken apart again if required. The join is visible on close inspection by design. Clamping pressure was kept deliberately light.

## Item 10
Cleaning removed adhesive residue from old tape from the linen sampler with a buffered enzyme solution, working in one direction only. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. A small window was left untouched for future reference.

## Item 11
Treatment of the flaking paint on the silver caddy spoon used a cellulose ether gel, applied under a heated spatula. Testing preceded each change of material. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 12
The wool tapestry is displayed at fifty-five per cent relative humidity under a hundred and fifty lux limit. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck.
