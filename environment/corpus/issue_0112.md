Collections Care Digest, issue 112

## Item 1
Treatment of the flaking paint on the oak chest used a cellulose ether gel, applied by nebuliser in several passes. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Testing preceded each change of material.

## Item 2
Cleaning removed hard calcareous crusts from the lacquer writing box with a rigid chelating gel, working in one direction only. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. A small window was left untouched for future reference.

## Item 3
Infrared spectroscopy of the oak chest identified bast fibre in the warp and protein fibre in the weft. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram.

## Item 4
Cleaning removed a discoloured natural resin coating from the plaster wall relief with swabs of a hydrocarbon and alcohol blend, working in short overlapping passes. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 5
Treatment of the flaking paint on the silver caddy spoon used a dilute acrylic resin, applied by nebuliser in several passes. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal.

## Item 6
The papier-mache tea tray was rehoused in a barrier-film enclosure with an oxygen scavenger, padded with polyester wadding. The mount maker was consulted before the design was fixed. Handling is now possible without touching the surface. The fit was adjusted twice before it was accepted.

## Item 7
A matte cellulose ether film was applied to the marble portrait bust following removal of the earlier coating. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area.

## Item 8
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the silk altar frontal used a wheat starch paste, applied under gentle vacuum suction. Testing preceded each change of material. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal.

## Item 9
Cleaning removed insect frass from the leather bookbinding with swabs of a hydrocarbon and alcohol blend, working in one direction only. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water.

## Item 10
A brushed dammar varnish was applied to the oak chest following consolidation of the powdering surface. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one.

## Item 11
Treatment of the cupped and tented ground on the plaster wall relief used a dilute acrylic resin, applied with a fine brush under magnification. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. The treated area was left under light weight overnight.

## Item 12
The pewter tankard is displayed at forty-five per cent relative humidity under a two hundred lux limit with annual rotation. The specification was agreed with the curator. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes.
