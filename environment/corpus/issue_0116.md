Collections Care Digest, issue 116

## Item 1
A brushed dammar varnish was applied to the lacquer writing box following toning of the fills with dry pigment. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area. The finish was left to cure for a week before display.

## Item 2
Cleaning removed greasy handling grime from the plaster wall relief with swabs of a hydrocarbon and alcohol blend, working in short overlapping passes. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water.

## Item 3
Raman microscopy of the linen sampler identified a reworked limb position in the underdrawing. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sampling was held to the minimum needed.

## Item 4
Cleaning removed greasy handling grime from the linen sampler with a buffered enzyme solution, working with frequent renewal of the swab. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage.

## Item 5
Treatment of the powdering pigment on the lacquer writing box used a dilute acrylic resin, applied under gentle vacuum suction. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Testing preceded each change of material.

## Item 6
The papier-mache tea tray is displayed at forty-five per cent relative humidity under a hundred and fifty lux limit. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. The specification was agreed with the curator.

## Item 7
X-ray fluorescence of the wool tapestry identified bast fibre in the warp and protein fibre in the weft. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sampling was held to the minimum needed.

## Item 8
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the vellum choir book used a microcrystalline wax, applied under a heated spatula. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Testing preceded each change of material.

## Item 9
Cleaning removed hard calcareous crusts from the vellum choir book with a rigid chelating gel, working in one direction only. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 10
The wool tapestry was rehoused in a coated brass tension strap, padded with silk crepeline. The fit was adjusted twice before it was accepted. The housing carries a printed handling instruction. The mount maker was consulted before the design was fixed.

## Item 11
Cleaning removed greasy handling grime from the oak chest with a rigid chelating gel, working in one direction only. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage.

## Item 12
Infrared spectroscopy of the leather bookbinding identified a mercury-based red beneath a later overpaint. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Results were checked against laboratory reference material.
