Conservation Notes, issue 121

## Item 1
Infrared spectroscopy of the glass roundel identified an arsenic-bearing green in the border. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sampling was held to the minimum needed.

## Item 2
Cleaning removed insect frass from the vellum choir book with grated vinyl crumb, working in short overlapping passes. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water.

## Item 3
Cleaning removed a discoloured natural resin coating from the leather bookbinding with grated vinyl crumb, working in short overlapping passes. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. A small window was left untouched for future reference.

## Item 4
The glass roundel was rehoused in a carved inert foam cradle, padded with unbuffered tissue. The housing carries a printed handling instruction. The mount maker was consulted before the design was fixed. Handling is now possible without touching the surface.

## Item 5
Treatment of the powdering pigment on the marble portrait bust used a dilute acrylic resin, applied under gentle vacuum suction. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Testing preceded each change of material. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 6
Cleaning removed adhesive residue from old tape from the glass roundel with a rigid chelating gel, working with frequent renewal of the swab. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 7
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the linen sampler used an isinglass solution, applied with a fine brush under magnification. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Testing preceded each change of material. The treated area was left under light weight overnight.

## Item 8
Cleaning removed sooty deposits from the glass roundel with a pulsed laser at low fluence, working with frequent renewal of the swab. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water.

## Item 9
Cleaning removed adhesive residue from old tape from the leather bookbinding with swabs of a hydrocarbon and alcohol blend, working in one direction only. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. A small window was left untouched for future reference.

## Item 10
Treatment of the powdering pigment on the ivory hair comb used an isinglass solution, applied under gentle vacuum suction. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Testing preceded each change of material.

## Item 11
The ivory hair comb is displayed at thirty-five per cent relative humidity under a hundred and fifty lux limit. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter. The specification was agreed with the curator.

## Item 12
Treatment of the powdering pigment on the ivory hair comb used an isinglass solution, applied under a heated spatula. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Testing preceded each change of material. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.
