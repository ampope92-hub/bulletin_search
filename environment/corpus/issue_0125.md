Studio Bulletin, issue 125

## Item 1
Cleaning removed greasy handling grime from the wool tapestry with a rigid chelating gel, working with frequent renewal of the swab. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage.

## Item 2
A failed earlier repair in the glass roundel was rejoined with a reversible animal glue. Alignment was dry-fitted before any adhesive was introduced. Clamping pressure was kept deliberately light. The repair can be taken apart again if required.

## Item 3
The vellum choir book was rehoused in a drop-back clamshell box, padded with unbuffered tissue. The housing carries a printed handling instruction. The mount maker was consulted before the design was fixed. The fit was adjusted twice before it was accepted.

## Item 4
The silk altar frontal is displayed at sixty per cent relative humidity under filtered daylight only. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. The specification was agreed with the curator.

## Item 5
Electron microscopy of a cross-section of the marble portrait bust identified a double ground of chalk under lead white. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. Results were checked against laboratory reference material.

## Item 6
Cleaning removed sooty deposits from the linen sampler with a rigid chelating gel, working with frequent renewal of the swab. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water.

## Item 7
Treatment of the flaking paint on the bronze votive figure used a dilute acrylic resin, applied by capillary feed along the edge. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Testing preceded each change of material. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal.

## Item 8
Treatment of the flaking paint on the silver caddy spoon used a cellulose ether gel, applied through fine tissue. Testing preceded each change of material. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 9
A matte cellulose ether film was applied to the lacquer writing box following consolidation of the powdering surface. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure.

## Item 10
X-ray fluorescence of the marble portrait bust identified a mercury-based red beneath a later overpaint. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. Sampling was held to the minimum needed.

## Item 11
The linen sampler is displayed at thirty-five per cent relative humidity under a fifty lux limit. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes.

## Item 12
The oak chest is displayed at thirty-five per cent relative humidity under a hundred and fifty lux limit. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes.
