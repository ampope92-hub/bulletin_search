Studio Bulletin, issue 127

## Item 1
X-ray fluorescence of the silver caddy spoon identified an arsenic-bearing green in the border. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. The finding is consistent with the attributed date.

## Item 2
Cleaning removed adhesive residue from old tape from the leather bookbinding with a buffered enzyme solution, working in one direction only. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage.

## Item 3
The glass roundel is displayed at sixty per cent relative humidity under filtered daylight only. The specification was agreed with the curator. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter.

## Item 4
Treatment of the powdering pigment on the plaster wall relief used a dilute acrylic resin, applied through fine tissue. Testing preceded each change of material. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 5
Cleaning removed a discoloured natural resin coating from the plaster wall relief with swabs of a hydrocarbon and alcohol blend, working in short overlapping passes. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 6
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the wool tapestry used a cellulose ether gel, applied under a heated spatula. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Testing preceded each change of material. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 7
A buffed microcrystalline wax was applied to the plaster wall relief following consolidation of the powdering surface. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one.

## Item 8
Polarised light microscopy of the silver caddy spoon identified a double ground of chalk under lead white. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. Results were checked against laboratory reference material.

## Item 9
A failed earlier repair in the lacquer writing box was rejoined with hidden hardwood pegs. The repair can be taken apart again if required. Clamping pressure was kept deliberately light. Alignment was dry-fitted before any adhesive was introduced.

## Item 10
Treatment of the crizzled surface on the glass roundel used a dilute acrylic resin, applied with a fine brush under magnification. Testing preceded each change of material. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 11
Cleaning removed hard calcareous crusts from the vellum choir book with swabs of a hydrocarbon and alcohol blend, working in one direction only. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. A small window was left untouched for future reference.

## Item 12
A matte cellulose ether film was applied to the bronze votive figure following removal of the earlier coating. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one.
