Studio Bulletin, issue 139

## Item 1
Cleaning removed sooty deposits from the linen sampler with swabs of a hydrocarbon and alcohol blend, working with frequent renewal of the swab. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water.

## Item 2
The ivory hair comb is displayed at forty-five per cent relative humidity under filtered daylight only. The specification was agreed with the curator. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter.

## Item 3
Treatment of the blistered varnish on the ivory hair comb used a cellulose ether gel, applied through fine tissue. Testing preceded each change of material. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal.

## Item 4
The leather bookbinding was rehoused in a drop-back clamshell box, padded with washed cotton jersey. The housing carries a printed handling instruction. The fit was adjusted twice before it was accepted. Handling is now possible without touching the surface.

## Item 5
A detached handle in the bronze votive figure was rejoined with a pin of matching alloy. Alignment was dry-fitted before any adhesive was introduced. The join is visible on close inspection by design. Clamping pressure was kept deliberately light.

## Item 6
The ivory hair comb was rehoused in a carved inert foam cradle, padded with polyester wadding. The fit was adjusted twice before it was accepted. The mount maker was consulted before the design was fixed. The housing carries a printed handling instruction.

## Item 7
Raman microscopy of the marble portrait bust identified a mercury-based red beneath a later overpaint. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. The finding is consistent with the attributed date.

## Item 8
Treatment of the cracked gilding on the papier-mache tea tray used a wheat starch paste, applied by capillary feed along the edge. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Testing preceded each change of material.

## Item 9
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the wool tapestry used a wheat starch paste, applied under a heated spatula. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Testing preceded each change of material. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 10
The ivory hair comb was rehoused in a carved inert foam cradle, padded with washed cotton jersey. The fit was adjusted twice before it was accepted. The mount maker was consulted before the design was fixed. Handling is now possible without touching the surface.

## Item 11
A failed earlier repair in the leather bookbinding was rejoined with thread-by-thread bridging. Clamping pressure was kept deliberately light. The repair can be taken apart again if required. Alignment was dry-fitted before any adhesive was introduced.

## Item 12
Treatment of the flaking paint on the marble portrait bust used a lime nanoparticle dispersion, applied by capillary feed along the edge. Testing preceded each change of material. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. The treated area was left under light weight overnight.
