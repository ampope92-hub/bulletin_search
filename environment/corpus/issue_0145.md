Conservation Notes, issue 145

## Item 1
Infrared spectroscopy of the lacquer writing box identified an arsenic-bearing green in the border. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram.

## Item 2
Cleaning removed adhesive residue from old tape from the linen sampler with a buffered enzyme solution, working in one direction only. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. A small window was left untouched for future reference.

## Item 3
Raman microscopy of the bronze votive figure identified a double ground of chalk under lead white. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Sampling was held to the minimum needed.

## Item 4
Cleaning removed a discoloured natural resin coating from the silver caddy spoon with a pulsed laser at low fluence, working from the least sensitive area outward. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. A small window was left untouched for future reference.

## Item 5
A failed earlier repair in the linen sampler was rejoined with thread-by-thread bridging. The join is visible on close inspection by design. Alignment was dry-fitted before any adhesive was introduced. The repair can be taken apart again if required.

## Item 6
Cleaning removed greasy handling grime from the bronze votive figure with a pulsed laser at low fluence, working in one direction only. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water.

## Item 7
Cleaning removed adhesive residue from old tape from the marble portrait bust with a pulsed laser at low fluence, working with frequent renewal of the swab. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water.

## Item 8
The pewter tankard is displayed at fifty-five per cent relative humidity under filtered daylight only. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes.

## Item 9
Treatment of the crizzled surface on the glass roundel used a microcrystalline wax, applied through fine tissue. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. The treated area was left under light weight overnight.

## Item 10
A sprayed low-molecular-weight resin was applied to the linen sampler following consolidation of the powdering surface. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area.

## Item 11
A buffed microcrystalline wax was applied to the oak chest following removal of the earlier coating. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area. The finish was left to cure for a week before display.

## Item 12
Radiography of the marble portrait bust identified a double ground of chalk under lead white. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. The finding is consistent with the attributed date.
