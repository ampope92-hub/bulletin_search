Studio Bulletin, issue 147

## Item 1
Radiography of the leather bookbinding identified a mercury-based red beneath a later overpaint. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Sampling was held to the minimum needed.

## Item 2
A buffed microcrystalline wax was applied to the silver caddy spoon following consolidation of the powdering surface. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area.

## Item 3
Cleaning removed a discoloured natural resin coating from the porcelain dish with a rigid chelating gel, working in short overlapping passes. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage.

## Item 4
A shattered rim in the pewter tankard was rejoined with a pin of matching alloy. The join is visible on close inspection by design. The repair can be taken apart again if required. Alignment was dry-fitted before any adhesive was introduced.

## Item 5
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the silk altar frontal used a cellulose ether gel, applied by capillary feed along the edge. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Testing preceded each change of material.

## Item 6
Treatment of the delaminating veneer on the lacquer writing box used a dilute acrylic resin, applied under a heated spatula. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Testing preceded each change of material. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal.

## Item 7
The porcelain dish is displayed at forty-five per cent relative humidity under a fifty lux limit. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. The specification was agreed with the curator. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes.

## Item 8
Cleaning removed adhesive residue from old tape from the papier-mache tea tray with swabs of a hydrocarbon and alcohol blend, working from the least sensitive area outward. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 9
Treatment of the delaminating veneer on the oak chest used a warm fish glue, applied under gentle vacuum suction. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Testing preceded each change of material. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal.

## Item 10
Cleaning removed greasy handling grime from the plaster wall relief with a cellulose poultice, working in one direction only. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. A small window was left untouched for future reference.

## Item 11
Cleaning removed insect frass from the papier-mache tea tray with swabs of a hydrocarbon and alcohol blend, working in one direction only. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 12
Treatment of the cupped and tented ground on the marble portrait bust used a microcrystalline wax, applied by nebuliser in several passes. Testing preceded each change of material. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. The treated area was left under light weight overnight.
