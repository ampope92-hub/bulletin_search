Studio Bulletin, issue 154

## Item 1
Infrared spectroscopy of the vellum choir book identified a drying oil bound with a little pine resin. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. The finding is consistent with the attributed date.

## Item 2
Treatment of the crizzled surface on the glass roundel used a dilute acrylic resin, applied with a fine brush under magnification. Testing preceded each change of material. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 3
A brushed dammar varnish was applied to the plaster wall relief following toning of the fills with dry pigment. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area. The finish was left to cure for a week before display.

## Item 4
Treatment of the powdering pigment on the lacquer writing box used a dilute acrylic resin, applied by capillary feed along the edge. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal.

## Item 5
Cleaning removed sooty deposits from the marble portrait bust with swabs of a hydrocarbon and alcohol blend, working in one direction only. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. A small window was left untouched for future reference.

## Item 6
Treatment of the blistered varnish on the papier-mache tea tray used a cellulose ether gel, applied with a fine brush under magnification. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 7
Electron microscopy of a cross-section of the papier-mache tea tray identified a reworked limb position in the underdrawing. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram.

## Item 8
Treatment of the powdering pigment on the papier-mache tea tray used a warm fish glue, applied with a fine brush under magnification. Testing preceded each change of material. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal.

## Item 9
Cleaning removed insect frass from the linen sampler with swabs of a hydrocarbon and alcohol blend, working in short overlapping passes. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage.

## Item 10
The pewter tankard is displayed at fifty-five per cent relative humidity under a fifty lux limit. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes.

## Item 11
Electron microscopy of a cross-section of the glass roundel identified a reworked limb position in the underdrawing. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram.

## Item 12
A through crack in the oak chest was rejoined with a reversible animal glue. The repair can be taken apart again if required. Alignment was dry-fitted before any adhesive was introduced. The join is visible on close inspection by design.
