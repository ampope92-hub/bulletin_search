Conservation Notes, issue 156

## Item 1
Infrared spectroscopy of the plaster wall relief identified a mercury-based red beneath a later overpaint. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram.

## Item 2
A sprayed low-molecular-weight resin was applied to the silver caddy spoon following consolidation of the powdering surface. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure.

## Item 3
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the wool tapestry used a seaweed-derived size, applied through fine tissue. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Testing preceded each change of material. The treated area was left under light weight overnight.

## Item 4
Cleaning removed hard calcareous crusts from the silk altar frontal with a buffered enzyme solution, working with frequent renewal of the swab. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage.

## Item 5
Cleaning removed hard calcareous crusts from the silk altar frontal with a rigid chelating gel, working with frequent renewal of the swab. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. A small window was left untouched for future reference.

## Item 6
The lacquer writing box is displayed at thirty-five per cent relative humidity under a fifty lux limit. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter. The specification was agreed with the curator.

## Item 7
Cleaning removed greasy handling grime from the pewter tankard with a pulsed laser at low fluence, working in short overlapping passes. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 8
Treatment of the flaking paint on the papier-mache tea tray used a seaweed-derived size, applied by capillary feed along the edge. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Testing preceded each change of material. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 9
The silk altar frontal is displayed at fifty-five per cent relative humidity under filtered daylight only. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes. The specification was agreed with the curator. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter.

## Item 10
Treatment of the flaking paint on the plaster wall relief used a microcrystalline wax, applied through fine tissue. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Testing preceded each change of material.

## Item 11
Raman microscopy of the wool tapestry identified a double ground of chalk under lead white. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram.

## Item 12
Cleaning removed hard calcareous crusts from the glass roundel with a pulsed laser at low fluence, working from the least sensitive area outward. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.
