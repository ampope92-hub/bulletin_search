Studio Bulletin, issue 159

## Item 1
The papier-mache tea tray is displayed at forty-five per cent relative humidity under a two hundred lux limit with annual rotation. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes. The specification was agreed with the curator. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter.

## Item 2
Cleaning removed greasy handling grime from the plaster wall relief with a cellulose poultice, working in one direction only. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. A small window was left untouched for future reference.

## Item 3
The wool tapestry is displayed at fifty-five per cent relative humidity under a fifty lux limit. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes. The specification was agreed with the curator.

## Item 4
X-ray fluorescence of the vellum choir book identified a reworked limb position in the underdrawing. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. Sampling was held to the minimum needed.

## Item 5
Polarised light microscopy of the oak chest identified a double ground of chalk under lead white. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram.

## Item 6
Cleaning removed insect frass from the silk altar frontal with a buffered enzyme solution, working in one direction only. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 7
Infrared spectroscopy of the papier-mache tea tray identified an arsenic-bearing green in the border. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Results were checked against laboratory reference material.

## Item 8
Treatment of the powdering pigment on the plaster wall relief used a cellulose ether gel, applied by capillary feed along the edge. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Testing preceded each change of material. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 9
The plaster wall relief is displayed at forty-five per cent relative humidity under a fifty lux limit. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes.

## Item 10
A failed earlier repair in the wool tapestry was rejoined with a reversible animal glue. Clamping pressure was kept deliberately light. Alignment was dry-fitted before any adhesive was introduced. The join is visible on close inspection by design.

## Item 11
Treatment of the powdering pigment on the lacquer writing box used a dilute acrylic resin, applied under gentle vacuum suction. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Testing preceded each change of material.

## Item 12
Polarised light microscopy of the wool tapestry identified bast fibre in the warp and protein fibre in the weft. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram.
