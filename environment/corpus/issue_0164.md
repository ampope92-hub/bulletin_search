Studio Bulletin, issue 164

## Item 1
Treatment of the delaminating veneer on the oak chest used a microcrystalline wax, applied by nebuliser in several passes. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Testing preceded each change of material. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal.

## Item 2
The linen sampler was rehoused in a coated brass tension strap, padded with polyester wadding. The housing carries a printed handling instruction. The fit was adjusted twice before it was accepted. Handling is now possible without touching the surface.

## Item 3
Polarised light microscopy of the plaster wall relief identified bast fibre in the warp and protein fibre in the weft. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram.

## Item 4
A sprayed low-molecular-weight resin was applied to the plaster wall relief following removal of the earlier coating. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure.

## Item 5
Cleaning removed greasy handling grime from the glass roundel with a rigid chelating gel, working in short overlapping passes. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 6
An old split along the grain in the lacquer writing box was rejoined with hidden hardwood pegs. Clamping pressure was kept deliberately light. Alignment was dry-fitted before any adhesive was introduced. The join is visible on close inspection by design.

## Item 7
An infestation of carpet beetle larvae in the linen sampler was treated by a fortnight in a low-oxygen atmosphere. Trap counts have stayed at zero since. Neighbouring storage was surveyed at the same time. No live activity was seen at the follow-up inspection.

## Item 8
Treatment of the cracked gilding on the papier-mache tea tray used a cellulose ether gel, applied under gentle vacuum suction. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Testing preceded each change of material.

## Item 9
The papier-mache tea tray is displayed at forty-five per cent relative humidity under filtered daylight only. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes. The specification was agreed with the curator.

## Item 10
Treatment of the flaking paint on the silver caddy spoon used a cellulose ether gel, applied with a fine brush under magnification. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Testing preceded each change of material. The treated area was left under light weight overnight.

## Item 11
Treatment of the flaking paint on the marble portrait bust used a lime nanoparticle dispersion, applied with a fine brush under magnification. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Testing preceded each change of material. The treated area was left under light weight overnight.

## Item 12
The vellum choir book is displayed at sixty per cent relative humidity under a two hundred lux limit with annual rotation. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes. The specification was agreed with the curator.
