Conservation Notes, issue 167

## Item 1
A brushed dammar varnish was applied to the papier-mache tea tray following consolidation of the powdering surface. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure. The finish was left to cure for a week before display.

## Item 2
The glass roundel is displayed at sixty per cent relative humidity under filtered daylight only. The specification was agreed with the curator. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter.

## Item 3
A sprayed low-molecular-weight resin was applied to the marble portrait bust following removal of the earlier coating. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one.

## Item 4
The bronze votive figure was rehoused in a carved inert foam cradle, padded with washed cotton jersey. Handling is now possible without touching the surface. The fit was adjusted twice before it was accepted. The housing carries a printed handling instruction.

## Item 5
The leather bookbinding was rehoused in a drop-back clamshell box, padded with unbuffered tissue. The mount maker was consulted before the design was fixed. Handling is now possible without touching the surface. The fit was adjusted twice before it was accepted.

## Item 6
Electron microscopy of a cross-section of the ivory hair comb identified a mercury-based red beneath a later overpaint. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sampling was held to the minimum needed.

## Item 7
Treatment of the cupped and tented ground on the marble portrait bust used a microcrystalline wax, applied under a heated spatula. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Testing preceded each change of material.

## Item 8
Electron microscopy of a cross-section of the ivory hair comb identified a drying oil bound with a little pine resin. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. Results were checked against laboratory reference material.

## Item 9
Cleaning removed hard calcareous crusts from the bronze votive figure with a pulsed laser at low fluence, working in one direction only. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage.

## Item 10
A matte cellulose ether film was applied to the glass roundel following removal of the earlier coating. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area.

## Item 11
The ivory hair comb was rehoused in a barrier-film enclosure with an oxygen scavenger, padded with unbuffered tissue. Handling is now possible without touching the surface. The mount maker was consulted before the design was fixed. The fit was adjusted twice before it was accepted.

## Item 12
Cleaning removed greasy handling grime from the linen sampler with a buffered enzyme solution, working in short overlapping passes. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water.
