Collections Care Digest, issue 168

## Item 1
A through crack in the marble portrait bust was rejoined with a reversible animal glue. The join is visible on close inspection by design. Alignment was dry-fitted before any adhesive was introduced. Clamping pressure was kept deliberately light.

## Item 2
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the linen sampler used a microcrystalline wax, applied under a heated spatula. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 3
Treatment of the blistered varnish on the papier-mache tea tray used an isinglass solution, applied under a heated spatula. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Testing preceded each change of material.

## Item 4
The leather bookbinding is displayed at forty-five per cent relative humidity under filtered daylight only. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes. The specification was agreed with the curator. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck.

## Item 5
Cleaning removed adhesive residue from old tape from the glass roundel with a cellulose poultice, working from the least sensitive area outward. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. A small window was left untouched for future reference.

## Item 6
An infestation of webbing clothes moth in the lacquer writing box was treated by localised heat treatment. No live activity was seen at the follow-up inspection. Neighbouring storage was surveyed at the same time. Trap counts have stayed at zero since.

## Item 7
Treatment of the flaking paint on the silver caddy spoon used a cellulose ether gel, applied under a heated spatula. Testing preceded each change of material. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 8
Infrared spectroscopy of the glass roundel identified a mercury-based red beneath a later overpaint. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. The finding is consistent with the attributed date.

## Item 9
Treatment of the blistered varnish on the papier-mache tea tray used a microcrystalline wax, applied by nebuliser in several passes. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Testing preceded each change of material. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal.

## Item 10
Treatment of the flaking paint on the pewter tankard used a microcrystalline wax, applied by capillary feed along the edge. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Testing preceded each change of material.

## Item 11
A sprayed low-molecular-weight resin was applied to the papier-mache tea tray following toning of the fills with dry pigment. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one.

## Item 12
Treatment of the powdering pigment on the plaster wall relief used a microcrystalline wax, applied by capillary feed along the edge. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.
