Studio Bulletin, issue 169

## Item 1
Treatment of the cupped and tented ground on the lacquer writing box used a dilute acrylic resin, applied through fine tissue. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Testing preceded each change of material.

## Item 2
The porcelain dish is displayed at forty-five per cent relative humidity under filtered daylight only. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter. The specification was agreed with the curator. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes.

## Item 3
Treatment of the cupped and tented ground on the plaster wall relief used a dilute acrylic resin, applied by nebuliser in several passes. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Testing preceded each change of material. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal.

## Item 4
Treatment of the blistered varnish on the oak chest used a cellulose ether gel, applied under a heated spatula. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Testing preceded each change of material. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 5
A sprayed low-molecular-weight resin was applied to the marble portrait bust following removal of the earlier coating. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one.

## Item 6
Cleaning removed a discoloured natural resin coating from the wool tapestry with swabs of a hydrocarbon and alcohol blend, working in short overlapping passes. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage.

## Item 7
Cleaning removed a discoloured natural resin coating from the papier-mache tea tray with a rigid chelating gel, working in one direction only. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water.

## Item 8
Cleaning removed a discoloured natural resin coating from the papier-mache tea tray with swabs of a hydrocarbon and alcohol blend, working in one direction only. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 9
The leather bookbinding is displayed at fifty-five per cent relative humidity under a fifty lux limit. The specification was agreed with the curator. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes.

## Item 10
A sprayed low-molecular-weight resin was applied to the vellum choir book following removal of the earlier coating. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure.

## Item 11
A matte cellulose ether film was applied to the bronze votive figure following consolidation of the powdering surface. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one.

## Item 12
Treatment of the powdering pigment on the papier-mache tea tray used a microcrystalline wax, applied with a fine brush under magnification. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Testing preceded each change of material.
