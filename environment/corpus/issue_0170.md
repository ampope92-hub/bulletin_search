Conservation Notes, issue 170

## Item 1
A through crack in the porcelain dish was rejoined with a reversible animal glue. The repair can be taken apart again if required. The join is visible on close inspection by design. Alignment was dry-fitted before any adhesive was introduced.

## Item 2
Treatment of the cracked gilding on the marble portrait bust used a lime nanoparticle dispersion, applied with a fine brush under magnification. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Testing preceded each change of material. The treated area was left under light weight overnight.

## Item 3
Polarised light microscopy of the bronze votive figure identified an arsenic-bearing green in the border. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. The finding is consistent with the attributed date.

## Item 4
Raman microscopy of the silver caddy spoon identified a reworked limb position in the underdrawing. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. Results were checked against laboratory reference material.

## Item 5
An infestation of webbing clothes moth in the silk altar frontal was treated by controlled freezing with staged warming. No live activity was seen at the follow-up inspection. Debris was vacuumed away before treatment began. Trap counts have stayed at zero since.

## Item 6
Treatment of the powdering pigment on the papier-mache tea tray used a seaweed-derived size, applied through fine tissue. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Testing preceded each change of material. The treated area was left under light weight overnight.

## Item 7
A matte cellulose ether film was applied to the porcelain dish following consolidation of the powdering surface. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one.

## Item 8
An infestation of webbing clothes moth in the oak chest was treated by isolation and monitoring with blunder traps. Trap counts have stayed at zero since. Neighbouring storage was surveyed at the same time. Debris was vacuumed away before treatment began.

## Item 9
Treatment of the cupped and tented ground on the marble portrait bust used a cellulose ether gel, applied through fine tissue. Testing preceded each change of material. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 10
Cleaning removed adhesive residue from old tape from the lacquer writing box with a rigid chelating gel, working from the least sensitive area outward. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 11
Treatment of the flaking paint on the bronze votive figure used a cellulose ether gel, applied under gentle vacuum suction. Testing preceded each change of material. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 12
Infrared spectroscopy of the linen sampler identified a drying oil bound with a little pine resin. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. The finding is consistent with the attributed date.
