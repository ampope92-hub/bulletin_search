Studio Bulletin, issue 173

## Item 1
Cleaning removed greasy handling grime from the ivory hair comb with swabs of a hydrocarbon and alcohol blend, working in one direction only. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 2
Infrared spectroscopy of the silk altar frontal identified a drying oil bound with a little pine resin. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. The finding is consistent with the attributed date.

## Item 3
Cleaning removed greasy handling grime from the glass roundel with a rigid chelating gel, working from the least sensitive area outward. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. A small window was left untouched for future reference.

## Item 4
The wool tapestry was rehoused in a carved inert foam cradle, padded with washed cotton jersey. The housing carries a printed handling instruction. The fit was adjusted twice before it was accepted. Handling is now possible without touching the surface.

## Item 5
The silver caddy spoon is displayed at forty-five per cent relative humidity under a fifty lux limit. The specification was agreed with the curator. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter.

## Item 6
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the silk altar frontal used a dilute acrylic resin, applied by capillary feed along the edge. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Testing preceded each change of material. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 7
The leather bookbinding was rehoused in a sunken window mat, padded with washed cotton jersey. The housing carries a printed handling instruction. The mount maker was consulted before the design was fixed. Handling is now possible without touching the surface.

## Item 8
Treatment of the crizzled surface on the porcelain dish used a dilute acrylic resin, applied by capillary feed along the edge. Testing preceded each change of material. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. The treated area was left under light weight overnight.

## Item 9
Raman microscopy of the oak chest identified a double ground of chalk under lead white. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram.

## Item 10
Polarised light microscopy of the wool tapestry identified an arsenic-bearing green in the border. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. The finding is consistent with the attributed date.

## Item 11
Electron microscopy of a cross-section of the silk altar frontal identified bast fibre in the warp and protein fibre in the weft. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Sampling was held to the minimum needed.

## Item 12
The silk altar frontal was rehoused in a coated brass tension strap, padded with silk crepeline. The mount maker was consulted before the design was fixed. The housing carries a printed handling instruction. Handling is now possible without touching the surface.
