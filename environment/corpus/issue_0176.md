Collections Care Digest, issue 176

## Item 1
Cleaning removed hard calcareous crusts from the glass roundel with a rigid chelating gel, working in short overlapping passes. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 2
The lacquer writing box is displayed at forty-five per cent relative humidity under a fifty lux limit. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. The specification was agreed with the curator. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes.

## Item 3
A through crack in the silver caddy spoon was rejoined with a pin of matching alloy. The repair can be taken apart again if required. Clamping pressure was kept deliberately light. The join is visible on close inspection by design.

## Item 4
Treatment of the cracked gilding on the ivory hair comb used an isinglass solution, applied by nebuliser in several passes. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Testing preceded each change of material.

## Item 5
Electron microscopy of a cross-section of the oak chest identified bast fibre in the warp and protein fibre in the weft. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Results were checked against laboratory reference material.

## Item 6
The lacquer writing box was rehoused in a carved inert foam cradle, padded with washed cotton jersey. The housing carries a printed handling instruction. Handling is now possible without touching the surface. The mount maker was consulted before the design was fixed.

## Item 7
Cleaning removed a discoloured natural resin coating from the linen sampler with a rigid chelating gel, working from the least sensitive area outward. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water.

## Item 8
A matte cellulose ether film was applied to the porcelain dish following removal of the earlier coating. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one.

## Item 9
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the leather bookbinding used a seaweed-derived size, applied by capillary feed along the edge. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. The treated area was left under light weight overnight.

## Item 10
Radiography of the ivory hair comb identified a double ground of chalk under lead white. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. The finding is consistent with the attributed date.

## Item 11
Cleaning removed hard calcareous crusts from the glass roundel with a pulsed laser at low fluence, working with frequent renewal of the swab. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. A small window was left untouched for future reference.

## Item 12
The plaster wall relief is displayed at fifty-five per cent relative humidity under a two hundred lux limit with annual rotation. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck.
