Studio Bulletin, issue 186

## Item 1
The silk altar frontal was rehoused in a coated brass tension strap, padded with washed cotton jersey. The mount maker was consulted before the design was fixed. Handling is now possible without touching the surface. The housing carries a printed handling instruction.

## Item 2
A brushed dammar varnish was applied to the oak chest following removal of the earlier coating. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area. The finish was left to cure for a week before display.

## Item 3
An infestation of carpet beetle larvae in the silk altar frontal was treated by controlled freezing with staged warming. Trap counts have stayed at zero since. No live activity was seen at the follow-up inspection. Neighbouring storage was surveyed at the same time.

## Item 4
Radiography of the bronze votive figure identified a double ground of chalk under lead white. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. Results were checked against laboratory reference material.

## Item 5
The lacquer writing box is displayed at sixty per cent relative humidity under a hundred and fifty lux limit. The specification was agreed with the curator. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck.

## Item 6
Cleaning removed insect frass from the silk altar frontal with a rigid chelating gel, working from the least sensitive area outward. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 7
Treatment of the flaking paint on the ivory hair comb used a seaweed-derived size, applied through fine tissue. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Testing preceded each change of material. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 8
Treatment of the blistered varnish on the lacquer writing box used a cellulose ether gel, applied under gentle vacuum suction. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. The treated area was left under light weight overnight.

## Item 9
Cleaning removed hard calcareous crusts from the glass roundel with a pulsed laser at low fluence, working from the least sensitive area outward. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 10
X-ray fluorescence of the silk altar frontal identified an arsenic-bearing green in the border. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Sampling was held to the minimum needed.

## Item 11
Treatment of the delaminating veneer on the lacquer writing box used a microcrystalline wax, applied by capillary feed along the edge. Testing preceded each change of material. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. The treated area was left under light weight overnight.

## Item 12
A sprayed low-molecular-weight resin was applied to the vellum choir book following removal of the earlier coating. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure.
