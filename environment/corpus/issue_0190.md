Conservation Notes, issue 190

## Item 1
Raman microscopy of the ivory hair comb identified an arsenic-bearing green in the border. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. The finding is consistent with the attributed date.

## Item 2
A matte cellulose ether film was applied to the linen sampler following removal of the earlier coating. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one.

## Item 3
Treatment of the powdering pigment on the lacquer writing box used a warm fish glue, applied by nebuliser in several passes. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Testing preceded each change of material. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal.

## Item 4
Cleaning removed hard calcareous crusts from the bronze votive figure with a pulsed laser at low fluence, working in one direction only. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage.

## Item 5
Raman microscopy of the papier-mache tea tray identified a double ground of chalk under lead white. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. Results were checked against laboratory reference material.

## Item 6
Cleaning removed a discoloured natural resin coating from the marble portrait bust with a rigid chelating gel, working in short overlapping passes. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water.

## Item 7
A sprayed low-molecular-weight resin was applied to the oak chest following removal of the earlier coating. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one.

## Item 8
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the wool tapestry used a warm fish glue, applied under a heated spatula. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Testing preceded each change of material. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 9
Treatment of the powdering pigment on the plaster wall relief used a dilute acrylic resin, applied through fine tissue. Testing preceded each change of material. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 10
A through crack in the glass roundel was rejoined with a reversible animal glue. The repair can be taken apart again if required. The join is visible on close inspection by design. Alignment was dry-fitted before any adhesive was introduced.

## Item 11
An infestation of silverfish in the papier-mache tea tray was treated by localised heat treatment. No live activity was seen at the follow-up inspection. Debris was vacuumed away before treatment began. Trap counts have stayed at zero since.

## Item 12
Treatment of the flaking paint on the bronze votive figure used a microcrystalline wax, applied under a heated spatula. Testing preceded each change of material. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.
