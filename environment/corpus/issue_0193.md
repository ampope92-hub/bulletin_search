Collections Care Digest, issue 193

## Item 1
Cleaning removed hard calcareous crusts from the silk altar frontal with a buffered enzyme solution, working in short overlapping passes. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 2
A detached handle in the porcelain dish was rejoined with a reversible animal glue. The join is visible on close inspection by design. Alignment was dry-fitted before any adhesive was introduced. Clamping pressure was kept deliberately light.

## Item 3
Cleaning removed hard calcareous crusts from the bronze votive figure with a pulsed laser at low fluence, working from the least sensitive area outward. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage.

## Item 4
Cleaning removed adhesive residue from old tape from the silk altar frontal with a rigid chelating gel, working from the least sensitive area outward. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. A small window was left untouched for future reference.

## Item 5
X-ray fluorescence of the papier-mache tea tray identified an arsenic-bearing green in the border. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. Results were checked against laboratory reference material.

## Item 6
Treatment of the flaking paint on the silver caddy spoon used a cellulose ether gel, applied by nebuliser in several passes. Testing preceded each change of material. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 7
Cleaning removed greasy handling grime from the glass roundel with a pulsed laser at low fluence, working from the least sensitive area outward. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water.

## Item 8
A matte cellulose ether film was applied to the linen sampler following toning of the fills with dry pigment. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure.

## Item 9
Electron microscopy of a cross-section of the vellum choir book identified an arsenic-bearing green in the border. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram.

## Item 10
Cleaning removed sooty deposits from the papier-mache tea tray with grated vinyl crumb, working in short overlapping passes. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 11
A failed earlier repair in the linen sampler was rejoined with a reversible animal glue. The join is visible on close inspection by design. Alignment was dry-fitted before any adhesive was introduced. The repair can be taken apart again if required.

## Item 12
The glass roundel is displayed at thirty-five per cent relative humidity under a hundred and fifty lux limit. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. The specification was agreed with the curator.
