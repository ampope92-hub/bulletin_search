Conservation Notes, issue 197

## Item 1
Treatment of the cupped and tented ground on the lacquer writing box used a dilute acrylic resin, applied through fine tissue. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Testing preceded each change of material.

## Item 2
The silver caddy spoon is displayed at forty-five per cent relative humidity under filtered daylight only. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. The specification was agreed with the curator. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes.

## Item 3
The bronze votive figure was rehoused in a barrier-film enclosure with an oxygen scavenger, padded with washed cotton jersey. Handling is now possible without touching the surface. The housing carries a printed handling instruction. The mount maker was consulted before the design was fixed.

## Item 4
Cleaning removed greasy handling grime from the lacquer writing box with swabs of a hydrocarbon and alcohol blend, working with frequent renewal of the swab. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. A small window was left untouched for future reference.

## Item 5
Cleaning removed sooty deposits from the marble portrait bust with a pulsed laser at low fluence, working from the least sensitive area outward. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. A small window was left untouched for future reference.

## Item 6
Polarised light microscopy of the wool tapestry identified bast fibre in the warp and protein fibre in the weft. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram.

## Item 7
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the silk altar frontal used a warm fish glue, applied under gentle vacuum suction. Testing preceded each change of material. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal.

## Item 8
Cleaning removed adhesive residue from old tape from the silver caddy spoon with a pulsed laser at low fluence, working from the least sensitive area outward. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 9
Treatment of the flaking paint on the oak chest used a warm fish glue, applied by capillary feed along the edge. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Testing preceded each change of material. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 10
Electron microscopy of a cross-section of the plaster wall relief identified a drying oil bound with a little pine resin. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram.

## Item 11
Treatment of the powdering pigment on the ivory hair comb used an isinglass solution, applied under gentle vacuum suction. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Testing preceded each change of material.

## Item 12
Treatment of the cracked gilding on the lacquer writing box used a warm fish glue, applied under a heated spatula. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Testing preceded each change of material.
