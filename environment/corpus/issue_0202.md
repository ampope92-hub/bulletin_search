Conservation Notes, issue 202

## Item 1
Treatment of the blistered varnish on the papier-mache tea tray used a cellulose ether gel, applied through fine tissue. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Testing preceded each change of material.

## Item 2
Treatment of the flaking paint on the bronze votive figure used a dilute acrylic resin, applied under gentle vacuum suction. Testing preceded each change of material. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 3
Cleaning removed hard calcareous crusts from the bronze votive figure with a pulsed laser at low fluence, working with frequent renewal of the swab. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 4
Raman microscopy of the silver caddy spoon identified a double ground of chalk under lead white. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram.

## Item 5
A sprayed low-molecular-weight resin was applied to the papier-mache tea tray following toning of the fills with dry pigment. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one.

## Item 6
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the linen sampler used a seaweed-derived size, applied under a heated spatula. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Testing preceded each change of material. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal.

## Item 7
Raman microscopy of the papier-mache tea tray identified a drying oil bound with a little pine resin. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. Results were checked against laboratory reference material.

## Item 8
Radiography of the pewter tankard identified an arsenic-bearing green in the border. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. The finding is consistent with the attributed date.

## Item 9
Infrared spectroscopy of the bronze votive figure identified a drying oil bound with a little pine resin. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. The finding is consistent with the attributed date.

## Item 10
Cleaning removed adhesive residue from old tape from the leather bookbinding with grated vinyl crumb, working in short overlapping passes. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. A small window was left untouched for future reference.

## Item 11
The vellum choir book is displayed at fifty-five per cent relative humidity under filtered daylight only. The specification was agreed with the curator. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck.

## Item 12
Cleaning removed insect frass from the papier-mache tea tray with a rigid chelating gel, working with frequent renewal of the swab. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.
