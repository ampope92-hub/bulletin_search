Collections Care Digest, issue 204

## Item 1
Cleaning removed a discoloured natural resin coating from the oak chest with a rigid chelating gel, working with frequent renewal of the swab. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage.

## Item 2
The papier-mache tea tray is displayed at sixty per cent relative humidity under a fifty lux limit. The specification was agreed with the curator. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes.

## Item 3
The linen sampler is displayed at thirty-five per cent relative humidity under a hundred and fifty lux limit. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes. The specification was agreed with the curator.

## Item 4
Cleaning removed adhesive residue from old tape from the pewter tankard with a pulsed laser at low fluence, working from the least sensitive area outward. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water.

## Item 5
The oak chest is displayed at forty-five per cent relative humidity under filtered daylight only. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes.

## Item 6
Treatment of the flaking paint on the plaster wall relief used a microcrystalline wax, applied through fine tissue. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Testing preceded each change of material.

## Item 7
Treatment of the powdering pigment on the ivory hair comb used an isinglass solution, applied under a heated spatula. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Testing preceded each change of material. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 8
A buffed microcrystalline wax was applied to the lacquer writing box following removal of the earlier coating. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area. The finish was left to cure for a week before display.

## Item 9
The linen sampler was rehoused in a coated brass tension strap, padded with polyester wadding. The housing carries a printed handling instruction. The fit was adjusted twice before it was accepted. Handling is now possible without touching the surface.

## Item 10
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the vellum choir book used an isinglass solution, applied through fine tissue. Testing preceded each change of material. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal.

## Item 11
Treatment of the cupped and tented ground on the plaster wall relief used a microcrystalline wax, applied by nebuliser in several passes. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Testing preceded each change of material.

## Item 12
Treatment of the flaking paint on the ivory hair comb used a cellulose ether gel, applied by capillary feed along the edge. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Testing preceded each change of material. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal.
