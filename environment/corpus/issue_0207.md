Conservation Notes, issue 207

## Item 1
Cleaning removed adhesive residue from old tape from the porcelain dish with a pulsed laser at low fluence, working in one direction only. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water.

## Item 2
Cleaning removed a discoloured natural resin coating from the vellum choir book with a buffered enzyme solution, working with frequent renewal of the swab. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 3
Cleaning removed adhesive residue from old tape from the wool tapestry with a buffered enzyme solution, working with frequent renewal of the swab. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 4
The oak chest is displayed at sixty per cent relative humidity under a fifty lux limit. The specification was agreed with the curator. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck.

## Item 5
The linen sampler was rehoused in a carved inert foam cradle, padded with washed cotton jersey. The fit was adjusted twice before it was accepted. The mount maker was consulted before the design was fixed. The housing carries a printed handling instruction.

## Item 6
Treatment of the flaking paint on the marble portrait bust used a lime nanoparticle dispersion, applied with a fine brush under magnification. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Testing preceded each change of material. The treated area was left under light weight overnight.

## Item 7
Electron microscopy of a cross-section of the plaster wall relief identified bast fibre in the warp and protein fibre in the weft. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. The finding is consistent with the attributed date.

## Item 8
Treatment of the cupped and tented ground on the plaster wall relief used a microcrystalline wax, applied by nebuliser in several passes. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Testing preceded each change of material.

## Item 9
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the wool tapestry used a seaweed-derived size, applied through fine tissue. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Testing preceded each change of material. The treated area was left under light weight overnight.

## Item 10
Cleaning removed a discoloured natural resin coating from the plaster wall relief with a pulsed laser at low fluence, working from the least sensitive area outward. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 11
A matte cellulose ether film was applied to the papier-mache tea tray following consolidation of the powdering surface. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area.

## Item 12
Treatment of the flaking paint on the bronze votive figure used a cellulose ether gel, applied with a fine brush under magnification. Testing preceded each change of material. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal.
