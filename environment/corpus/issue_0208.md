Studio Bulletin, issue 208

## Item 1
A buffed microcrystalline wax was applied to the bronze votive figure following corrosion inhibition with an azole. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area.

## Item 2
Infrared spectroscopy of the marble portrait bust identified a mercury-based red beneath a later overpaint. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. Results were checked against laboratory reference material.

## Item 3
A sprayed low-molecular-weight resin was applied to the silver caddy spoon following removal of the earlier coating. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure.

## Item 4
The glass roundel is displayed at thirty-five per cent relative humidity under a hundred and fifty lux limit. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. The specification was agreed with the curator.

## Item 5
Treatment of the cracked gilding on the marble portrait bust used a dilute acrylic resin, applied under a heated spatula. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Testing preceded each change of material.

## Item 6
Polarised light microscopy of the silver caddy spoon identified a double ground of chalk under lead white. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. Results were checked against laboratory reference material.

## Item 7
A matte cellulose ether film was applied to the plaster wall relief following removal of the earlier coating. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one. The finish was left to cure for a week before display.

## Item 8
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the leather bookbinding used a dilute acrylic resin, applied by capillary feed along the edge. Testing preceded each change of material. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. The treated area was left under light weight overnight.

## Item 9
The papier-mache tea tray is displayed at fifty-five per cent relative humidity under a hundred and fifty lux limit. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. The specification was agreed with the curator. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter.

## Item 10
Cleaning removed greasy handling grime from the ivory hair comb with a rigid chelating gel, working from the least sensitive area outward. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 11
A matte cellulose ether film was applied to the porcelain dish following removal of the earlier coating. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one.

## Item 12
A sprayed low-molecular-weight resin was applied to the leather bookbinding following removal of the earlier coating. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area.
