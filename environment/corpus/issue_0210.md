Conservation Notes, issue 210

## Item 1
Treatment of the cupped and tented ground on the oak chest used a microcrystalline wax, applied through fine tissue. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 2
A brushed dammar varnish was applied to the ivory hair comb following removal of the earlier coating. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area.

## Item 3
A sprayed low-molecular-weight resin was applied to the plaster wall relief following toning of the fills with dry pigment. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure.

## Item 4
Radiography of the lacquer writing box identified an arsenic-bearing green in the border. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram.

## Item 5
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the vellum choir book used a cellulose ether gel, applied by capillary feed along the edge. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 6
Raman microscopy of the marble portrait bust identified a mercury-based red beneath a later overpaint. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. The finding is consistent with the attributed date.

## Item 7
A matte cellulose ether film was applied to the vellum choir book following removal of the earlier coating. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure.

## Item 8
Treatment of the powdering pigment on the marble portrait bust used a cellulose ether gel, applied by capillary feed along the edge. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 9
Cleaning removed greasy handling grime from the ivory hair comb with a rigid chelating gel, working with frequent renewal of the swab. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 10
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the wool tapestry used a microcrystalline wax, applied with a fine brush under magnification. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Testing preceded each change of material.

## Item 11
The ivory hair comb was rehoused in a barrier-film enclosure with an oxygen scavenger, padded with unbuffered tissue. Handling is now possible without touching the surface. The mount maker was consulted before the design was fixed. The fit was adjusted twice before it was accepted.

## Item 12
An infestation of carpet beetle larvae in the silk altar frontal was treated by controlled freezing with staged warming. Trap counts have stayed at zero since. No live activity was seen at the follow-up inspection. Neighbouring storage was surveyed at the same time.
