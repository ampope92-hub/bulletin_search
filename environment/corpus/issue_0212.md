Studio Bulletin, issue 212

## Item 1
A sprayed low-molecular-weight resin was applied to the ivory hair comb following toning of the fills with dry pigment. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure.

## Item 2
Cleaning removed greasy handling grime from the marble portrait bust with swabs of a hydrocarbon and alcohol blend, working with frequent renewal of the swab. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water.

## Item 3
Electron microscopy of a cross-section of the bronze votive figure identified bast fibre in the warp and protein fibre in the weft. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sampling was held to the minimum needed.

## Item 4
Electron microscopy of a cross-section of the linen sampler identified a drying oil bound with a little pine resin. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram.

## Item 5
The papier-mache tea tray was rehoused in a barrier-film enclosure with an oxygen scavenger, padded with polyester wadding. The mount maker was consulted before the design was fixed. Handling is now possible without touching the surface. The fit was adjusted twice before it was accepted.

## Item 6
A matte cellulose ether film was applied to the pewter tankard following consolidation of the powdering surface. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area.

## Item 7
Cleaning removed adhesive residue from old tape from the vellum choir book with grated vinyl crumb, working in one direction only. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water.

## Item 8
Treatment of the flaking paint on the oak chest used a warm fish glue, applied under gentle vacuum suction. Testing preceded each change of material. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. The treated area was left under light weight overnight.

## Item 9
A failed earlier repair in the pewter tankard was rejoined with a pin of matching alloy. The join is visible on close inspection by design. Clamping pressure was kept deliberately light. Alignment was dry-fitted before any adhesive was introduced.

## Item 10
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the leather bookbinding used an isinglass solution, applied under gentle vacuum suction. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Testing preceded each change of material. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal.

## Item 11
Electron microscopy of a cross-section of the silk altar frontal identified a mercury-based red beneath a later overpaint. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Results were checked against laboratory reference material.

## Item 12
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the linen sampler used a cellulose ether gel, applied by capillary feed along the edge. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Testing preceded each change of material. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.
