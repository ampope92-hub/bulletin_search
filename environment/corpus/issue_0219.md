Collections Care Digest, issue 219

## Item 1
A sprayed low-molecular-weight resin was applied to the silver caddy spoon following corrosion inhibition with an azole. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area.

## Item 2
Treatment of the flaking paint on the plaster wall relief used a cellulose ether gel, applied under gentle vacuum suction. Testing preceded each change of material. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal.

## Item 3
A matte cellulose ether film was applied to the linen sampler following removal of the earlier coating. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one.

## Item 4
Treatment of the cupped and tented ground on the plaster wall relief used a cellulose ether gel, applied under gentle vacuum suction. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal.

## Item 5
A through crack in the marble portrait bust was rejoined with a reversible animal glue. The join is visible on close inspection by design. Alignment was dry-fitted before any adhesive was introduced. Clamping pressure was kept deliberately light.

## Item 6
The pewter tankard is displayed at sixty per cent relative humidity under a two hundred lux limit with annual rotation. The specification was agreed with the curator. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes.

## Item 7
A through crack in the lacquer writing box was rejoined with a shaped balsa fill. The repair can be taken apart again if required. Alignment was dry-fitted before any adhesive was introduced. The join is visible on close inspection by design.

## Item 8
Cleaning removed adhesive residue from old tape from the oak chest with swabs of a hydrocarbon and alcohol blend, working in short overlapping passes. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage.

## Item 9
Cleaning removed hard calcareous crusts from the wool tapestry with swabs of a hydrocarbon and alcohol blend, working in one direction only. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 10
Raman microscopy of the wool tapestry identified a drying oil bound with a little pine resin. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. The finding is consistent with the attributed date.

## Item 11
Treatment of the cracked gilding on the oak chest used a cellulose ether gel, applied by capillary feed along the edge. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Testing preceded each change of material.

## Item 12
The vellum choir book is displayed at thirty-five per cent relative humidity under a fifty lux limit. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes.
