Studio Bulletin, issue 220

## Item 1
Treatment of the cracked gilding on the lacquer writing box used a warm fish glue, applied under a heated spatula. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Testing preceded each change of material.

## Item 2
The pewter tankard is displayed at forty-five per cent relative humidity under a hundred and fifty lux limit. The specification was agreed with the curator. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck.

## Item 3
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the silk altar frontal used a microcrystalline wax, applied by nebuliser in several passes. Testing preceded each change of material. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal.

## Item 4
Radiography of the wool tapestry identified a mercury-based red beneath a later overpaint. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Results were checked against laboratory reference material.

## Item 5
Cleaning removed greasy handling grime from the ivory hair comb with a rigid chelating gel, working in short overlapping passes. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water.

## Item 6
Electron microscopy of a cross-section of the bronze votive figure identified a reworked limb position in the underdrawing. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. The finding is consistent with the attributed date.

## Item 7
A matte cellulose ether film was applied to the silk altar frontal following consolidation of the powdering surface. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area.

## Item 8
Cleaning removed insect frass from the papier-mache tea tray with a rigid chelating gel, working in one direction only. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water.

## Item 9
Polarised light microscopy of the silk altar frontal identified a drying oil bound with a little pine resin. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sampling was held to the minimum needed.

## Item 10
The linen sampler is displayed at fifty-five per cent relative humidity under a hundred and fifty lux limit. The specification was agreed with the curator. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes.

## Item 11
The pewter tankard is displayed at fifty-five per cent relative humidity under filtered daylight only. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes.

## Item 12
Treatment of the powdering pigment on the marble portrait bust used a lime nanoparticle dispersion, applied under a heated spatula. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. The treated area was left under light weight overnight.
