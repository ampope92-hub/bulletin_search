Collections Care Digest, issue 5

## Item 1
Raman microscopy of the vellum choir book identified a double ground of chalk under lead white. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. The finding is consistent with the attributed date.

## Item 2
Cleaning removed adhesive residue from old tape from the oak chest with a rigid chelating gel, working from the least sensitive area outward. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. A small window was left untouched for future reference.

## Item 3
Electron microscopy of a cross-section of the wool tapestry identified a mercury-based red beneath a later overpaint. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Results were checked against laboratory reference material.

## Item 4
The ivory hair comb was rehoused in a carved inert foam cradle, padded with washed cotton jersey. The fit was adjusted twice before it was accepted. The mount maker was consulted before the design was fixed. Handling is now possible without touching the surface.

## Item 5
Infrared spectroscopy of the leather bookbinding identified an arsenic-bearing green in the border. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. The finding is consistent with the attributed date.

## Item 6
A failed earlier repair in the papier-mache tea tray was rejoined with a reversible animal glue. Clamping pressure was kept deliberately light. The join is visible on close inspection by design. Alignment was dry-fitted before any adhesive was introduced.

## Item 7
An infestation of silverfish in the papier-mache tea tray was treated by localised heat treatment. No live activity was seen at the follow-up inspection. Debris was vacuumed away before treatment began. Trap counts have stayed at zero since.

## Item 8
The vellum choir book was rehoused in a sunken window mat, padded with unbuffered tissue. The housing carries a printed handling instruction. The fit was adjusted twice before it was accepted. Handling is now possible without touching the surface.

## Item 9
Cleaning removed a discoloured natural resin coating from the leather bookbinding with a buffered enzyme solution, working in short overlapping passes. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. A small window was left untouched for future reference.

## Item 10
Treatment of the flaking paint on the papier-mache tea tray used a microcrystalline wax, applied under a heated spatula. Testing preceded each change of material. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. The treated area was left under light weight overnight.

## Item 11
Cleaning removed adhesive residue from old tape from the pewter tankard with a pulsed laser at low fluence, working from the least sensitive area outward. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water.

## Item 12
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the vellum choir book used a dilute acrylic resin, applied under gentle vacuum suction. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.
