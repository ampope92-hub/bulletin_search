Conservation Notes, issue 7

## Item 1
X-ray fluorescence of the pewter tankard identified a double ground of chalk under lead white. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram.

## Item 2
Cleaning removed sooty deposits from the linen sampler with a rigid chelating gel, working in one direction only. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 3
Cleaning removed sooty deposits from the papier-mache tea tray with grated vinyl crumb, working in one direction only. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water.

## Item 4
Cleaning removed insect frass from the papier-mache tea tray with swabs of a hydrocarbon and alcohol blend, working in one direction only. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 5
The silk altar frontal was rehoused in a coated brass tension strap, padded with polyester wadding. The fit was adjusted twice before it was accepted. The housing carries a printed handling instruction. The mount maker was consulted before the design was fixed.

## Item 6
Cleaning removed a discoloured natural resin coating from the plaster wall relief with a rigid chelating gel, working with frequent renewal of the swab. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage.

## Item 7
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the linen sampler used an isinglass solution, applied under a heated spatula. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Testing preceded each change of material.

## Item 8
The vellum choir book is displayed at thirty-five per cent relative humidity under a two hundred lux limit with annual rotation. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. The specification was agreed with the curator.

## Item 9
The oak chest is displayed at forty-five per cent relative humidity under a hundred and fifty lux limit. The specification was agreed with the curator. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck.

## Item 10
X-ray fluorescence of the wool tapestry identified a double ground of chalk under lead white. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. Sampling was held to the minimum needed.

## Item 11
Cleaning removed a discoloured natural resin coating from the papier-mache tea tray with grated vinyl crumb, working in short overlapping passes. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water.

## Item 12
A sprayed low-molecular-weight resin was applied to the vellum choir book following toning of the fills with dry pigment. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure. The finish was left to cure for a week before display.
