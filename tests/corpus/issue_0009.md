Studio Bulletin, issue 9

## Item 1
Raman microscopy of the vellum choir book identified a mercury-based red beneath a later overpaint. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. Results were checked against laboratory reference material.

## Item 2
Electron microscopy of a cross-section of the silver caddy spoon identified a double ground of chalk under lead white. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sampling was held to the minimum needed.

## Item 3
The vellum choir book was rehoused in a drop-back clamshell box, padded with unbuffered tissue. The housing carries a printed handling instruction. The mount maker was consulted before the design was fixed. The fit was adjusted twice before it was accepted.

## Item 4
Treatment of the cupped and tented ground on the marble portrait bust used a lime nanoparticle dispersion, applied by capillary feed along the edge. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Testing preceded each change of material.

## Item 5
The pewter tankard is displayed at forty-five per cent relative humidity under filtered daylight only. The specification was agreed with the curator. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter.

## Item 6
A shattered rim in the silver caddy spoon was rejoined with a pin of matching alloy. The repair can be taken apart again if required. Alignment was dry-fitted before any adhesive was introduced. The join is visible on close inspection by design.

## Item 7
Cleaning removed sooty deposits from the ivory hair comb with a rigid chelating gel, working in short overlapping passes. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 8
Cleaning removed greasy handling grime from the pewter tankard with a pulsed laser at low fluence, working from the least sensitive area outward. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. A small window was left untouched for future reference.

## Item 9
An infestation of webbing clothes moth in the lacquer writing box was treated by isolation and monitoring with blunder traps. Debris was vacuumed away before treatment began. No live activity was seen at the follow-up inspection. Neighbouring storage was surveyed at the same time.

## Item 10
An infestation of silverfish in the vellum choir book was treated by controlled freezing with staged warming. Neighbouring storage was surveyed at the same time. Trap counts have stayed at zero since. No live activity was seen at the follow-up inspection.

## Item 11
Cleaning removed insect frass from the leather bookbinding with a buffered enzyme solution, working in short overlapping passes. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 12
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the vellum choir book used a seaweed-derived size, applied by nebuliser in several passes. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal.
