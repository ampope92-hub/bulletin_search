Studio Bulletin, issue 12

## Item 1
Treatment of the powdering pigment on the papier-mache tea tray used a warm fish glue, applied with a fine brush under magnification. Testing preceded each change of material. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal.

## Item 2
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the wool tapestry used a cellulose ether gel, applied through fine tissue. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. The treated area was left under light weight overnight.

## Item 3
A sprayed low-molecular-weight resin was applied to the ivory hair comb following removal of the earlier coating. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure. The finish was left to cure for a week before display.

## Item 4
A buffed microcrystalline wax was applied to the plaster wall relief following toning of the fills with dry pigment. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure. The finish was left to cure for a week before display.

## Item 5
Polarised light microscopy of the glass roundel identified a double ground of chalk under lead white. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Sampling was held to the minimum needed.

## Item 6
Cleaning removed adhesive residue from old tape from the vellum choir book with swabs of a hydrocarbon and alcohol blend, working in one direction only. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage.

## Item 7
Cleaning removed insect frass from the vellum choir book with a rigid chelating gel, working in one direction only. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 8
Polarised light microscopy of the leather bookbinding identified a drying oil bound with a little pine resin. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sampling was held to the minimum needed.

## Item 9
Radiography of the lacquer writing box identified a double ground of chalk under lead white. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. Results were checked against laboratory reference material.

## Item 10
Cleaning removed adhesive residue from old tape from the plaster wall relief with swabs of a hydrocarbon and alcohol blend, working from the least sensitive area outward. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. A small window was left untouched for future reference.

## Item 11
Polarised light microscopy of the ivory hair comb identified a double ground of chalk under lead white. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. Sampling was held to the minimum needed.

## Item 12
Treatment of the flaking paint on the silver caddy spoon used a dilute acrylic resin, applied under a heated spatula. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Testing preceded each change of material. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.
