Conservation Notes, issue 13

## Item 1
The wool tapestry was rehoused in a carved inert foam cradle, padded with silk crepeline. The fit was adjusted twice before it was accepted. Handling is now possible without touching the surface. The mount maker was consulted before the design was fixed.

## Item 2
The leather bookbinding was rehoused in a carved inert foam cradle, padded with unbuffered tissue. The fit was adjusted twice before it was accepted. The mount maker was consulted before the design was fixed. The housing carries a printed handling instruction.

## Item 3
Cleaning removed a discoloured natural resin coating from the wool tapestry with swabs of a hydrocarbon and alcohol blend, working in short overlapping passes. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage.

## Item 4
The silk altar frontal was rehoused in a carved inert foam cradle, padded with unbuffered tissue. Handling is now possible without touching the surface. The housing carries a printed handling instruction. The fit was adjusted twice before it was accepted.

## Item 5
Infrared spectroscopy of the lacquer writing box identified a drying oil bound with a little pine resin. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. Sampling was held to the minimum needed.

## Item 6
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the silk altar frontal used a warm fish glue, applied under a heated spatula. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Testing preceded each change of material.

## Item 7
An infestation of silverfish in the papier-mache tea tray was treated by a fortnight in a low-oxygen atmosphere. Trap counts have stayed at zero since. Neighbouring storage was surveyed at the same time. Debris was vacuumed away before treatment began.

## Item 8
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the leather bookbinding used a wheat starch paste, applied through fine tissue. Testing preceded each change of material. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal.

## Item 9
A sprayed low-molecular-weight resin was applied to the linen sampler following removal of the earlier coating. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one. The finish was left to cure for a week before display.

## Item 10
The leather bookbinding was rehoused in a barrier-film enclosure with an oxygen scavenger, padded with unbuffered tissue. The fit was adjusted twice before it was accepted. The mount maker was consulted before the design was fixed. Handling is now possible without touching the surface.

## Item 11
X-ray fluorescence of the marble portrait bust identified a reworked limb position in the underdrawing. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Results were checked against laboratory reference material.
