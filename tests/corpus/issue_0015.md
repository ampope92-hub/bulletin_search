Collections Care Digest, issue 15

## Item 1
The linen sampler is displayed at thirty-five per cent relative humidity under filtered daylight only. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes.

## Item 2
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the leather bookbinding used a seaweed-derived size, applied through fine tissue. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Testing preceded each change of material.

## Item 3
A sprayed low-molecular-weight resin was applied to the glass roundel following removal of the earlier coating. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure.

## Item 4
Treatment of the cracked gilding on the papier-mache tea tray used a wheat starch paste, applied by capillary feed along the edge. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Testing preceded each change of material.

## Item 5
Treatment of the flaking paint on the silver caddy spoon used a cellulose ether gel, applied under gentle vacuum suction. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. The treated area was left under light weight overnight.

## Item 6
X-ray fluorescence of the pewter tankard identified a mercury-based red beneath a later overpaint. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. Sampling was held to the minimum needed.

## Item 7
Treatment of the flaking paint on the bronze votive figure used a dilute acrylic resin, applied through fine tissue. Testing preceded each change of material. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal.

## Item 8
A shattered rim in the bronze votive figure was rejoined with a pin of matching alloy. Alignment was dry-fitted before any adhesive was introduced. Clamping pressure was kept deliberately light. The join is visible on close inspection by design.

## Item 9
Cleaning removed hard calcareous crusts from the ivory hair comb with a rigid chelating gel, working in one direction only. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water.

## Item 10
The silver caddy spoon is displayed at forty-five per cent relative humidity under filtered daylight only. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. The specification was agreed with the curator. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes.

## Item 11
The leather bookbinding was rehoused in a carved inert foam cradle, padded with washed cotton jersey. Handling is now possible without touching the surface. The housing carries a printed handling instruction. The mount maker was consulted before the design was fixed.

## Item 12
X-ray fluorescence of the ivory hair comb identified bast fibre in the warp and protein fibre in the weft. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sampling was held to the minimum needed.
