Conservation Notes, issue 16

## Item 1
An infestation of silverfish in the ivory hair comb was treated by localised heat treatment. No live activity was seen at the follow-up inspection. Neighbouring storage was surveyed at the same time. Debris was vacuumed away before treatment began.

## Item 2
An infestation of silverfish in the vellum choir book was treated by isolation and monitoring with blunder traps. Debris was vacuumed away before treatment began. Trap counts have stayed at zero since. No live activity was seen at the follow-up inspection.

## Item 3
Cleaning removed hard calcareous crusts from the plaster wall relief with swabs of a hydrocarbon and alcohol blend, working in one direction only. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage.

## Item 4
A brushed dammar varnish was applied to the plaster wall relief following toning of the fills with dry pigment. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area. The finish was left to cure for a week before display.

## Item 5
The silver caddy spoon is displayed at thirty-five per cent relative humidity under filtered daylight only. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes. The specification was agreed with the curator. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck.

## Item 6
Treatment of the cupped and tented ground on the plaster wall relief used a lime nanoparticle dispersion, applied through fine tissue. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal.

## Item 7
Cleaning removed greasy handling grime from the ivory hair comb with a rigid chelating gel, working with frequent renewal of the swab. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 8
Electron microscopy of a cross-section of the plaster wall relief identified bast fibre in the warp and protein fibre in the weft. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. The finding is consistent with the attributed date.

## Item 9
Treatment of the delaminating veneer on the oak chest used a dilute acrylic resin, applied under gentle vacuum suction. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Testing preceded each change of material. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal.

## Item 10
Polarised light microscopy of the marble portrait bust identified a double ground of chalk under lead white. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. The finding is consistent with the attributed date.

## Item 11
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the silk altar frontal used a wheat starch paste, applied by nebuliser in several passes. Testing preceded each change of material. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 12
X-ray fluorescence of the papier-mache tea tray identified an arsenic-bearing green in the border. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. Results were checked against laboratory reference material.
