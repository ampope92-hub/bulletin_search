Conservation Notes, issue 22

## Item 1
Cleaning removed insect frass from the leather bookbinding with a buffered enzyme solution, working in short overlapping passes. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 2
Cleaning removed adhesive residue from old tape from the glass roundel with a rigid chelating gel, working with frequent renewal of the swab. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 3
Treatment of the cupped and tented ground on the plaster wall relief used a dilute acrylic resin, applied by capillary feed along the edge. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 4
Cleaning removed hard calcareous crusts from the leather bookbinding with swabs of a hydrocarbon and alcohol blend, working in one direction only. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage.

## Item 5
Infrared spectroscopy of the lacquer writing box identified a mercury-based red beneath a later overpaint. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. The finding is consistent with the attributed date.

## Item 6
Raman microscopy of the lacquer writing box identified an arsenic-bearing green in the border. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. The finding is consistent with the attributed date.

## Item 7
The leather bookbinding is displayed at fifty-five per cent relative humidity under filtered daylight only. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes. The specification was agreed with the curator.

## Item 8
The glass roundel is displayed at fifty-five per cent relative humidity under filtered daylight only. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes. The specification was agreed with the curator.

## Item 9
The linen sampler was rehoused in a coated brass tension strap, padded with unbuffered tissue. The fit was adjusted twice before it was accepted. The housing carries a printed handling instruction. The mount maker was consulted before the design was fixed.

## Item 10
A failed earlier repair in the lacquer writing box was rejoined with hidden hardwood pegs. The repair can be taken apart again if required. Clamping pressure was kept deliberately light. Alignment was dry-fitted before any adhesive was introduced.

## Item 11
Treatment of the flaking paint on the plaster wall relief used a microcrystalline wax, applied through fine tissue. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Testing preceded each change of material.

## Item 12
Raman microscopy of the papier-mache tea tray identified a drying oil bound with a little pine resin. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. Results were checked against laboratory reference material.
