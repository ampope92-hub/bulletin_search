Collections Care Digest, issue 28

## Item 1
Treatment of the crizzled surface on the glass roundel used a microcrystalline wax, applied by capillary feed along the edge. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. The treated area was left under light weight overnight.

## Item 2
A failed earlier repair in the ivory hair comb was rejoined with a reversible animal glue. The join is visible on close inspection by design. Alignment was dry-fitted before any adhesive was introduced. Clamping pressure was kept deliberately light.

## Item 3
The silk altar frontal is displayed at fifty-five per cent relative humidity under filtered daylight only. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes. The specification was agreed with the curator. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter.

## Item 4
Infrared spectroscopy of the wool tapestry identified a mercury-based red beneath a later overpaint. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. The finding is consistent with the attributed date.

## Item 5
Cleaning removed adhesive residue from old tape from the vellum choir book with swabs of a hydrocarbon and alcohol blend, working from the least sensitive area outward. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water.

## Item 6
X-ray fluorescence of the silver caddy spoon identified a drying oil bound with a little pine resin. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram.

## Item 7
Treatment of the flaking paint on the lacquer writing box used a cellulose ether gel, applied under gentle vacuum suction. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 8
Cleaning removed greasy handling grime from the papier-mache tea tray with a rigid chelating gel, working with frequent renewal of the swab. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage.

## Item 9
Polarised light microscopy of the silver caddy spoon identified a reworked limb position in the underdrawing. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. The finding is consistent with the attributed date.

## Item 10
The linen sampler was rehoused in a carved inert foam cradle, padded with polyester wadding. The housing carries a printed handling instruction. Handling is now possible without touching the surface. The mount maker was consulted before the design was fixed.

## Item 11
The papier-mache tea tray is displayed at sixty per cent relative humidity under a two hundred lux limit with annual rotation. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. The specification was agreed with the curator. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes.

## Item 12
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the silk altar frontal used a wheat starch paste, applied with a fine brush under magnification. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Testing preceded each change of material. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal.
