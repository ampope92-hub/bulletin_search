Studio Bulletin, issue 37

## Item 1
A failed earlier repair in the oak chest was rejoined with a shaped balsa fill. The join is visible on close inspection by design. Clamping pressure was kept deliberately light. Alignment was dry-fitted before any adhesive was introduced.

## Item 2
The ivory hair comb is displayed at thirty-five per cent relative humidity under a hundred and fifty lux limit. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter. The specification was agreed with the curator.

## Item 3
Infrared spectroscopy of the plaster wall relief identified a mercury-based red beneath a later overpaint. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram.

## Item 4
Radiography of the pewter tankard identified a drying oil bound with a little pine resin. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram.

## Item 5
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the wool tapestry used a cellulose ether gel, applied under a heated spatula. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Testing preceded each change of material. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 6
Cleaning removed sooty deposits from the leather bookbinding with a buffered enzyme solution, working with frequent renewal of the swab. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage.

## Item 7
Cleaning removed a discoloured natural resin coating from the bronze votive figure with swabs of a hydrocarbon and alcohol blend, working in one direction only. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. A small window was left untouched for future reference.

## Item 8
Treatment of the cracked gilding on the oak chest used a microcrystalline wax, applied with a fine brush under magnification. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Testing preceded each change of material.

## Item 9
Treatment of the powdering pigment on the ivory hair comb used a seaweed-derived size, applied by nebuliser in several passes. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Testing preceded each change of material. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal.

## Item 10
Cleaning removed adhesive residue from old tape from the vellum choir book with a rigid chelating gel, working from the least sensitive area outward. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 11
The leather bookbinding is displayed at thirty-five per cent relative humidity under filtered daylight only. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes. The specification was agreed with the curator. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter.

## Item 12
A matte cellulose ether film was applied to the vellum choir book following toning of the fills with dry pigment. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one.
