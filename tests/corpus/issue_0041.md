Conservation Notes, issue 41

## Item 1
A sprayed low-molecular-weight resin was applied to the pewter tankard following corrosion inhibition with an azole. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area.

## Item 2
X-ray fluorescence of the linen sampler identified bast fibre in the warp and protein fibre in the weft. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. The finding is consistent with the attributed date.

## Item 3
X-ray fluorescence of the pewter tankard identified an arsenic-bearing green in the border. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. Sampling was held to the minimum needed.

## Item 4
The pewter tankard is displayed at thirty-five per cent relative humidity under a hundred and fifty lux limit. The specification was agreed with the curator. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes.

## Item 5
Cleaning removed sooty deposits from the lacquer writing box with a rigid chelating gel, working with frequent renewal of the swab. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 6
Treatment of the flaking paint on the oak chest used a microcrystalline wax, applied under gentle vacuum suction. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Testing preceded each change of material. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal.

## Item 7
The silk altar frontal is displayed at sixty per cent relative humidity under a hundred and fifty lux limit. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes.

## Item 8
A detached handle in the silver caddy spoon was rejoined with a reversible animal glue. Clamping pressure was kept deliberately light. Alignment was dry-fitted before any adhesive was introduced. The join is visible on close inspection by design.

## Item 9
A matte cellulose ether film was applied to the plaster wall relief following toning of the fills with dry pigment. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area.

## Item 10
The ivory hair comb was rehoused in a barrier-film enclosure with an oxygen scavenger, padded with polyester wadding. The housing carries a printed handling instruction. Handling is now possible without touching the surface. The fit was adjusted twice before it was accepted.

## Item 11
Cleaning removed greasy handling grime from the silver caddy spoon with a pulsed laser at low fluence, working with frequent renewal of the swab. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water.

## Item 12
A brushed dammar varnish was applied to the marble portrait bust following consolidation of the powdering surface. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure.
