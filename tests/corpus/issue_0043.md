Collections Care Digest, issue 43

## Item 1
A buffed microcrystalline wax was applied to the bronze votive figure following consolidation of the powdering surface. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one.

## Item 2
The porcelain dish is displayed at fifty-five per cent relative humidity under filtered daylight only. The specification was agreed with the curator. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck.

## Item 3
A matte cellulose ether film was applied to the oak chest following consolidation of the powdering surface. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one. The finish was left to cure for a week before display.

## Item 4
Treatment of the delaminating veneer on the oak chest used a microcrystalline wax, applied by capillary feed along the edge. Testing preceded each change of material. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. The treated area was left under light weight overnight.

## Item 5
Raman microscopy of the wool tapestry identified an arsenic-bearing green in the border. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Results were checked against laboratory reference material.

## Item 6
Electron microscopy of a cross-section of the leather bookbinding identified a mercury-based red beneath a later overpaint. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sampling was held to the minimum needed.

## Item 7
Polarised light microscopy of the silver caddy spoon identified a drying oil bound with a little pine resin. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Sampling was held to the minimum needed.

## Item 8
Treatment of the powdering pigment on the marble portrait bust used a lime nanoparticle dispersion, applied by capillary feed along the edge. Testing preceded each change of material. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 9
Infrared spectroscopy of the oak chest identified an arsenic-bearing green in the border. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram.

## Item 10
Treatment of the cracked gilding on the lacquer writing box used a cellulose ether gel, applied through fine tissue. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. The treated area was left under light weight overnight.

## Item 11
Cleaning removed a discoloured natural resin coating from the bronze votive figure with a pulsed laser at low fluence, working from the least sensitive area outward. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. A small window was left untouched for future reference.

## Item 12
A buffed microcrystalline wax was applied to the pewter tankard following removal of the earlier coating. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area. The finish was left to cure for a week before display.
