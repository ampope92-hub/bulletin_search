Conservation Notes, issue 46

## Item 1
Cleaning removed hard calcareous crusts from the oak chest with a rigid chelating gel, working in short overlapping passes. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water.

## Item 2
An infestation of webbing clothes moth in the lacquer writing box was treated by localised heat treatment. No live activity was seen at the follow-up inspection. Neighbouring storage was surveyed at the same time. Trap counts have stayed at zero since.

## Item 3
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the linen sampler used a microcrystalline wax, applied with a fine brush under magnification. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Testing preceded each change of material.

## Item 4
An infestation of silverfish in the vellum choir book was treated by controlled freezing with staged warming. Neighbouring storage was surveyed at the same time. Trap counts have stayed at zero since. No live activity was seen at the follow-up inspection.

## Item 5
A matte cellulose ether film was applied to the wool tapestry following consolidation of the powdering surface. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one.

## Item 6
Treatment of the crizzled surface on the glass roundel used a dilute acrylic resin, applied under gentle vacuum suction. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Testing preceded each change of material. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal.

## Item 7
Cleaning removed sooty deposits from the glass roundel with swabs of a hydrocarbon and alcohol blend, working with frequent renewal of the swab. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. A small window was left untouched for future reference.

## Item 8
A matte cellulose ether film was applied to the plaster wall relief following toning of the fills with dry pigment. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area.

## Item 9
Treatment of the powdering pigment on the papier-mache tea tray used a cellulose ether gel, applied under gentle vacuum suction. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 10
The pewter tankard is displayed at fifty-five per cent relative humidity under a two hundred lux limit with annual rotation. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. The specification was agreed with the curator. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes.

## Item 11
Cleaning removed adhesive residue from old tape from the oak chest with a rigid chelating gel, working in short overlapping passes. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water.

## Item 12
Cleaning removed insect frass from the vellum choir book with a rigid chelating gel, working in one direction only. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.
