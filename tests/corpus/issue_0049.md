Conservation Notes, issue 49

## Item 1
Cleaning removed insect frass from the ivory hair comb with swabs of a hydrocarbon and alcohol blend, working in short overlapping passes. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage.

## Item 2
The porcelain dish is displayed at thirty-five per cent relative humidity under filtered daylight only. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes.

## Item 3
The leather bookbinding was rehoused in a drop-back clamshell box, padded with washed cotton jersey. The housing carries a printed handling instruction. The fit was adjusted twice before it was accepted. Handling is now possible without touching the surface.

## Item 4
The silk altar frontal was rehoused in a carved inert foam cradle, padded with polyester wadding. The mount maker was consulted before the design was fixed. The housing carries a printed handling instruction. Handling is now possible without touching the surface.

## Item 5
The leather bookbinding is displayed at forty-five per cent relative humidity under filtered daylight only. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes. The specification was agreed with the curator. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck.

## Item 6
An infestation of webbing clothes moth in the wool tapestry was treated by controlled freezing with staged warming. Trap counts have stayed at zero since. Neighbouring storage was surveyed at the same time. Debris was vacuumed away before treatment began.

## Item 7
Polarised light microscopy of the ivory hair comb identified a reworked limb position in the underdrawing. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram.

## Item 8
The leather bookbinding is displayed at thirty-five per cent relative humidity under a hundred and fifty lux limit. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes. The specification was agreed with the curator.

## Item 9
Cleaning removed hard calcareous crusts from the vellum choir book with swabs of a hydrocarbon and alcohol blend, working from the least sensitive area outward. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. A small window was left untouched for future reference.

## Item 10
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the leather bookbinding used a seaweed-derived size, applied by capillary feed along the edge. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. The treated area was left under light weight overnight.

## Item 11
A shattered rim in the porcelain dish was rejoined with a reversible animal glue. The repair can be taken apart again if required. The join is visible on close inspection by design. Alignment was dry-fitted before any adhesive was introduced.

## Item 12
Treatment of the powdering pigment on the lacquer writing box used a microcrystalline wax, applied through fine tissue. Testing preceded each change of material. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.
