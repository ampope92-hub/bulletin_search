Studio Bulletin, issue 52

## Item 1
A sprayed low-molecular-weight resin was applied to the silk altar frontal following removal of the earlier coating. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area. The finish was left to cure for a week before display.

## Item 2
Treatment of the cupped and tented ground on the marble portrait bust used a cellulose ether gel, applied by capillary feed along the edge. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Testing preceded each change of material. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 3
A sprayed low-molecular-weight resin was applied to the oak chest following consolidation of the powdering surface. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure. The finish was left to cure for a week before display.

## Item 4
Radiography of the silver caddy spoon identified a drying oil bound with a little pine resin. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Sampling was held to the minimum needed.

## Item 5
Cleaning removed hard calcareous crusts from the linen sampler with a buffered enzyme solution, working from the least sensitive area outward. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 6
Polarised light microscopy of the silk altar frontal identified a mercury-based red beneath a later overpaint. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Sampling was held to the minimum needed.

## Item 7
Cleaning removed greasy handling grime from the porcelain dish with a cellulose poultice, working in one direction only. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water.

## Item 8
The plaster wall relief is displayed at thirty-five per cent relative humidity under a two hundred lux limit with annual rotation. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes. The specification was agreed with the curator.

## Item 9
The lacquer writing box is displayed at forty-five per cent relative humidity under filtered daylight only. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter. The specification was agreed with the curator. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes.

## Item 10
Radiography of the silver caddy spoon identified a mercury-based red beneath a later overpaint. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. The finding is consistent with the attributed date.

## Item 11
An infestation of carpet beetle larvae in the linen sampler was treated by localised heat treatment. No live activity was seen at the follow-up inspection. Neighbouring storage was surveyed at the same time. Debris was vacuumed away before treatment began.

## Item 12
Treatment of the powdering pigment on the oak chest used a microcrystalline wax, applied through fine tissue. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Testing preceded each change of material.
