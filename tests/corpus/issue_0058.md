Studio Bulletin, issue 58

## Item 1
A matte cellulose ether film was applied to the ivory hair comb following toning of the fills with dry pigment. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area.

## Item 2
Cleaning removed adhesive residue from old tape from the leather bookbinding with a rigid chelating gel, working from the least sensitive area outward. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 3
Treatment of the powdering pigment on the marble portrait bust used a cellulose ether gel, applied by capillary feed along the edge. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 4
A failed earlier repair in the leather bookbinding was rejoined with a reversible animal glue. The join is visible on close inspection by design. Clamping pressure was kept deliberately light. Alignment was dry-fitted before any adhesive was introduced.

## Item 5
A through crack in the lacquer writing box was rejoined with a shaped balsa fill. The repair can be taken apart again if required. Alignment was dry-fitted before any adhesive was introduced. The join is visible on close inspection by design.

## Item 6
Cleaning removed greasy handling grime from the porcelain dish with swabs of a hydrocarbon and alcohol blend, working in one direction only. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 7
An infestation of webbing clothes moth in the oak chest was treated by a fortnight in a low-oxygen atmosphere. Trap counts have stayed at zero since. No live activity was seen at the follow-up inspection. Debris was vacuumed away before treatment began.

## Item 8
An infestation of webbing clothes moth in the wool tapestry was treated by localised heat treatment. No live activity was seen at the follow-up inspection. Trap counts have stayed at zero since. Debris was vacuumed away before treatment began.

## Item 9
The plaster wall relief is displayed at fifty-five per cent relative humidity under a fifty lux limit. The specification was agreed with the curator. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter.

## Item 10
X-ray fluorescence of the lacquer writing box identified an arsenic-bearing green in the border. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram.

## Item 11
The wool tapestry was rehoused in a carved inert foam cradle, padded with polyester wadding. The mount maker was consulted before the design was fixed. The fit was adjusted twice before it was accepted. Handling is now possible without touching the surface.

## Item 12
A brushed dammar varnish was applied to the ivory hair comb following removal of the earlier coating. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area.
