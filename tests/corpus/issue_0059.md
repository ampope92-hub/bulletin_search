Collections Care Digest, issue 59

## Item 1
Treatment of the flaking paint on the pewter tankard used a microcrystalline wax, applied by nebuliser in several passes. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 2
Cleaning removed sooty deposits from the porcelain dish with a pulsed laser at low fluence, working from the least sensitive area outward. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. A small window was left untouched for future reference.

## Item 3
Cleaning removed a discoloured natural resin coating from the ivory hair comb with swabs of a hydrocarbon and alcohol blend, working in one direction only. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 4
Treatment of the crizzled surface on the porcelain dish used a dilute acrylic resin, applied under gentle vacuum suction. Testing preceded each change of material. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. The treated area was left under light weight overnight.

## Item 5
The pewter tankard is displayed at fifty-five per cent relative humidity under a two hundred lux limit with annual rotation. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. The specification was agreed with the curator. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes.

## Item 6
A buffed microcrystalline wax was applied to the pewter tankard following removal of the earlier coating. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area. The finish was left to cure for a week before display.

## Item 7
Cleaning removed adhesive residue from old tape from the linen sampler with a rigid chelating gel, working in one direction only. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water.

## Item 8
Treatment of the crizzled surface on the glass roundel used a microcrystalline wax, applied with a fine brush under magnification. Testing preceded each change of material. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. The treated area was left under light weight overnight.

## Item 9
The silver caddy spoon was rehoused in a carved inert foam cradle, padded with washed cotton jersey. The mount maker was consulted before the design was fixed. The housing carries a printed handling instruction. Handling is now possible without touching the surface.

## Item 10
Cleaning removed greasy handling grime from the marble portrait bust with swabs of a hydrocarbon and alcohol blend, working with frequent renewal of the swab. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water.

## Item 11
Cleaning removed sooty deposits from the vellum choir book with a buffered enzyme solution, working from the least sensitive area outward. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 12
Radiography of the oak chest identified a reworked limb position in the underdrawing. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram.
