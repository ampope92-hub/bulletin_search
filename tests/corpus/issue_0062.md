Conservation Notes, issue 62

## Item 1
A sprayed low-molecular-weight resin was applied to the papier-mache tea tray following toning of the fills with dry pigment. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one.

## Item 2
The linen sampler was rehoused in a carved inert foam cradle, padded with silk crepeline. Handling is now possible without touching the surface. The fit was adjusted twice before it was accepted. The housing carries a printed handling instruction.

## Item 3
The silk altar frontal is displayed at sixty per cent relative humidity under a hundred and fifty lux limit. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes.

## Item 4
Radiography of the oak chest identified an arsenic-bearing green in the border. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. Sampling was held to the minimum needed.

## Item 5
The linen sampler was rehoused in a coated brass tension strap, padded with unbuffered tissue. The fit was adjusted twice before it was accepted. The housing carries a printed handling instruction. The mount maker was consulted before the design was fixed.

## Item 6
Cleaning removed adhesive residue from old tape from the porcelain dish with a rigid chelating gel, working with frequent renewal of the swab. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 7
Cleaning removed adhesive residue from old tape from the bronze votive figure with a pulsed laser at low fluence, working with frequent renewal of the swab. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage.

## Item 8
X-ray fluorescence of the papier-mache tea tray identified a reworked limb position in the underdrawing. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram.

## Item 9
Treatment of the flaking paint on the ivory hair comb used a wheat starch paste, applied through fine tissue. Testing preceded each change of material. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. The treated area was left under light weight overnight.

## Item 10
Cleaning removed adhesive residue from old tape from the plaster wall relief with swabs of a hydrocarbon and alcohol blend, working from the least sensitive area outward. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. A small window was left untouched for future reference.

## Item 11
Treatment of the flaking paint on the silver caddy spoon used a cellulose ether gel, applied by capillary feed along the edge. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. The treated area was left under light weight overnight.

## Item 12
An infestation of carpet beetle larvae in the linen sampler was treated by localised heat treatment. No live activity was seen at the follow-up inspection. Neighbouring storage was surveyed at the same time. Debris was vacuumed away before treatment began.
