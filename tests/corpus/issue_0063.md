Studio Bulletin, issue 63

## Item 1
A through crack in the pewter tankard was rejoined with a pin of matching alloy. Alignment was dry-fitted before any adhesive was introduced. The join is visible on close inspection by design. The repair can be taken apart again if required.

## Item 2
Cleaning removed greasy handling grime from the silk altar frontal with swabs of a hydrocarbon and alcohol blend, working in short overlapping passes. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. A small window was left untouched for future reference.

## Item 3
The silk altar frontal was rehoused in a coated brass tension strap, padded with silk crepeline. The mount maker was consulted before the design was fixed. The housing carries a printed handling instruction. Handling is now possible without touching the surface.

## Item 4
A detached handle in the silver caddy spoon was rejoined with a reversible animal glue. Clamping pressure was kept deliberately light. Alignment was dry-fitted before any adhesive was introduced. The join is visible on close inspection by design.

## Item 5
Infrared spectroscopy of the silk altar frontal identified a mercury-based red beneath a later overpaint. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Results were checked against laboratory reference material.

## Item 6
Cleaning removed sooty deposits from the lacquer writing box with swabs of a hydrocarbon and alcohol blend, working from the least sensitive area outward. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water.

## Item 7
Cleaning removed sooty deposits from the linen sampler with a buffered enzyme solution, working from the least sensitive area outward. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 8
Cleaning removed adhesive residue from old tape from the ivory hair comb with swabs of a hydrocarbon and alcohol blend, working with frequent renewal of the swab. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. A small window was left untouched for future reference.

## Item 9
A failed earlier repair in the vellum choir book was rejoined with thread-by-thread bridging. The repair can be taken apart again if required. The join is visible on close inspection by design. Alignment was dry-fitted before any adhesive was introduced.

## Item 10
Treatment of the blistered varnish on the oak chest used a cellulose ether gel, applied with a fine brush under magnification. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 11
Treatment of the cracked gilding on the ivory hair comb used an isinglass solution, applied by nebuliser in several passes. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Testing preceded each change of material.
