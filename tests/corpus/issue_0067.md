Studio Bulletin, issue 67

## Item 1
The porcelain dish was rehoused in a carved inert foam cradle, padded with washed cotton jersey. The housing carries a printed handling instruction. The fit was adjusted twice before it was accepted. Handling is now possible without touching the surface.

## Item 2
A sprayed low-molecular-weight resin was applied to the pewter tankard following consolidation of the powdering surface. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure.

## Item 3
A matte cellulose ether film was applied to the bronze votive figure following toning of the fills with dry pigment. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area.

## Item 4
Radiography of the linen sampler identified a drying oil bound with a little pine resin. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram.

## Item 5
A sprayed low-molecular-weight resin was applied to the leather bookbinding following toning of the fills with dry pigment. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure.

## Item 6
A sprayed low-molecular-weight resin was applied to the vellum choir book following consolidation of the powdering surface. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one.

## Item 7
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the wool tapestry used a microcrystalline wax, applied under gentle vacuum suction. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 8
Polarised light microscopy of the ivory hair comb identified a reworked limb position in the underdrawing. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram.

## Item 9
X-ray fluorescence of the pewter tankard identified a double ground of chalk under lead white. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram.

## Item 10
Treatment of the delaminating veneer on the oak chest used a warm fish glue, applied through fine tissue. Testing preceded each change of material. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. The treated area was left under light weight overnight.

## Item 11
A brushed dammar varnish was applied to the plaster wall relief following consolidation of the powdering surface. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area.

## Item 12
Infrared spectroscopy of the leather bookbinding identified an arsenic-bearing green in the border. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. The finding is consistent with the attributed date.
