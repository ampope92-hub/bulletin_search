Studio Bulletin, issue 69

## Item 1
A matte cellulose ether film was applied to the vellum choir book following toning of the fills with dry pigment. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one.

## Item 2
The silver caddy spoon is displayed at forty-five per cent relative humidity under a two hundred lux limit with annual rotation. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck.

## Item 3
A matte cellulose ether film was applied to the bronze votive figure following toning of the fills with dry pigment. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area.

## Item 4
Cleaning removed sooty deposits from the glass roundel with a pulsed laser at low fluence, working in short overlapping passes. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water.

## Item 5
The silver caddy spoon is displayed at thirty-five per cent relative humidity under a hundred and fifty lux limit. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck.

## Item 6
The vellum choir book is displayed at thirty-five per cent relative humidity under a fifty lux limit. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes.

## Item 7
Treatment of the flaking paint on the bronze votive figure used a cellulose ether gel, applied under gentle vacuum suction. Testing preceded each change of material. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 8
A detached handle in the lacquer writing box was rejoined with a reversible animal glue. The join is visible on close inspection by design. Clamping pressure was kept deliberately light. Alignment was dry-fitted before any adhesive was introduced.

## Item 9
Cleaning removed hard calcareous crusts from the glass roundel with swabs of a hydrocarbon and alcohol blend, working with frequent renewal of the swab. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage.

## Item 10
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the silk altar frontal used a wheat starch paste, applied under gentle vacuum suction. Testing preceded each change of material. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal.

## Item 11
The ivory hair comb was rehoused in a carved inert foam cradle, padded with polyester wadding. The fit was adjusted twice before it was accepted. The mount maker was consulted before the design was fixed. The housing carries a printed handling instruction.

## Item 12
The pewter tankard is displayed at fifty-five per cent relative humidity under a two hundred lux limit with annual rotation. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. The specification was agreed with the curator. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes.
