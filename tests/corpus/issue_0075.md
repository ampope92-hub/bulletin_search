Studio Bulletin, issue 75

## Item 1
Cleaning removed sooty deposits from the pewter tankard with a pulsed laser at low fluence, working from the least sensitive area outward. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 2
Cleaning removed a discoloured natural resin coating from the silk altar frontal with a buffered enzyme solution, working with frequent renewal of the swab. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water.

## Item 3
X-ray fluorescence of the bronze votive figure identified a mercury-based red beneath a later overpaint. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram.

## Item 4
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the wool tapestry used a microcrystalline wax, applied under gentle vacuum suction. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 5
A sprayed low-molecular-weight resin was applied to the wool tapestry following consolidation of the powdering surface. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure. The finish was left to cure for a week before display.

## Item 6
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the silk altar frontal used a microcrystalline wax, applied by nebuliser in several passes. Testing preceded each change of material. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal.

## Item 7
A sprayed low-molecular-weight resin was applied to the oak chest following consolidation of the powdering surface. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure. The finish was left to cure for a week before display.

## Item 8
A failed earlier repair in the pewter tankard was rejoined with a pin of matching alloy. The join is visible on close inspection by design. Clamping pressure was kept deliberately light. Alignment was dry-fitted before any adhesive was introduced.

## Item 9
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the vellum choir book used an isinglass solution, applied under a heated spatula. Testing preceded each change of material. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal.

## Item 10
Radiography of the oak chest identified bast fibre in the warp and protein fibre in the weft. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Sampling was held to the minimum needed.

## Item 11
An infestation of powderpost beetle in the lacquer writing box was treated by a fortnight in a low-oxygen atmosphere. Trap counts have stayed at zero since. Neighbouring storage was surveyed at the same time. No live activity was seen at the follow-up inspection.

## Item 12
A failed earlier repair in the oak chest was rejoined with a shaped balsa fill. The join is visible on close inspection by design. Clamping pressure was kept deliberately light. Alignment was dry-fitted before any adhesive was introduced.
