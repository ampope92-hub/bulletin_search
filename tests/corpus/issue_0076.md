Conservation Notes, issue 76

## Item 1
A brushed dammar varnish was applied to the lacquer writing box following consolidation of the powdering surface. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area.

## Item 2
Raman microscopy of the bronze votive figure identified an arsenic-bearing green in the border. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Results were checked against laboratory reference material.

## Item 3
A buffed microcrystalline wax was applied to the marble portrait bust following removal of the earlier coating. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure. The finish was left to cure for a week before display.

## Item 4
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the silk altar frontal used a microcrystalline wax, applied through fine tissue. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 5
An infestation of silverfish in the papier-mache tea tray was treated by localised heat treatment. No live activity was seen at the follow-up inspection. Debris was vacuumed away before treatment began. Trap counts have stayed at zero since.

## Item 6
The silk altar frontal was rehoused in a carved inert foam cradle, padded with washed cotton jersey. Handling is now possible without touching the surface. The mount maker was consulted before the design was fixed. The housing carries a printed handling instruction.

## Item 7
The leather bookbinding is displayed at forty-five per cent relative humidity under a fifty lux limit. The specification was agreed with the curator. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter.

## Item 8
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the linen sampler used a microcrystalline wax, applied under a heated spatula. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 9
Treatment of the delaminating veneer on the oak chest used a dilute acrylic resin, applied under a heated spatula. Testing preceded each change of material. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 10
Radiography of the silver caddy spoon identified an arsenic-bearing green in the border. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. Sampling was held to the minimum needed.

## Item 11
Cleaning removed a discoloured natural resin coating from the bronze votive figure with swabs of a hydrocarbon and alcohol blend, working in one direction only. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. A small window was left untouched for future reference.

## Item 12
Radiography of the oak chest identified a mercury-based red beneath a later overpaint. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. The finding is consistent with the attributed date.
