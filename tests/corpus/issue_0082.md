Conservation Notes, issue 82

## Item 1
Treatment of the crizzled surface on the glass roundel used a microcrystalline wax, applied through fine tissue. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. The treated area was left under light weight overnight.

## Item 2
Radiography of the papier-mache tea tray identified a reworked limb position in the underdrawing. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. The finding is consistent with the attributed date.

## Item 3
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the wool tapestry used a wheat starch paste, applied under a heated spatula. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Testing preceded each change of material. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 4
Treatment of the cupped and tented ground on the lacquer writing box used a dilute acrylic resin, applied by capillary feed along the edge. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 5
Cleaning removed adhesive residue from old tape from the bronze votive figure with a pulsed laser at low fluence, working with frequent renewal of the swab. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage.

## Item 6
An infestation of silverfish in the papier-mache tea tray was treated by a fortnight in a low-oxygen atmosphere. Trap counts have stayed at zero since. Neighbouring storage was surveyed at the same time. Debris was vacuumed away before treatment began.

## Item 7
Cleaning removed sooty deposits from the pewter tankard with a pulsed laser at low fluence, working with frequent renewal of the swab. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 8
The vellum choir book is displayed at forty-five per cent relative humidity under a two hundred lux limit with annual rotation. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes. The specification was agreed with the curator. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter.

## Item 9
A sprayed low-molecular-weight resin was applied to the porcelain dish following toning of the fills with dry pigment. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area.

## Item 10
A matte cellulose ether film was applied to the ivory hair comb following consolidation of the powdering surface. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure. The finish was left to cure for a week before display.

## Item 11
The vellum choir book is displayed at thirty-five per cent relative humidity under a fifty lux limit. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes.

## Item 12
A detached handle in the lacquer writing box was rejoined with a shaped balsa fill. Alignment was dry-fitted before any adhesive was introduced. The repair can be taken apart again if required. Clamping pressure was kept deliberately light.
