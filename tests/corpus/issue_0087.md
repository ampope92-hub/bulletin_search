Studio Bulletin, issue 87

## Item 1
Radiography of the marble portrait bust identified bast fibre in the warp and protein fibre in the weft. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Results were checked against laboratory reference material.

## Item 2
The pewter tankard was rehoused in a barrier-film enclosure with an oxygen scavenger, padded with washed cotton jersey. The fit was adjusted twice before it was accepted. Handling is now possible without touching the surface. The housing carries a printed handling instruction.

## Item 3
A through crack in the oak chest was rejoined with a shaped balsa fill. The repair can be taken apart again if required. Alignment was dry-fitted before any adhesive was introduced. The join is visible on close inspection by design.

## Item 4
Electron microscopy of a cross-section of the silk altar frontal identified a double ground of chalk under lead white. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. The finding is consistent with the attributed date.

## Item 5
Cleaning removed insect frass from the vellum choir book with a rigid chelating gel, working in one direction only. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 6
Treatment of the cupped and tented ground on the lacquer writing box used a dilute acrylic resin, applied by capillary feed along the edge. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 7
Treatment of the powdering pigment on the marble portrait bust used a lime nanoparticle dispersion, applied through fine tissue. Testing preceded each change of material. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal.

## Item 8
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the linen sampler used a cellulose ether gel, applied through fine tissue. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Testing preceded each change of material.

## Item 9
Cleaning removed sooty deposits from the wool tapestry with swabs of a hydrocarbon and alcohol blend, working in one direction only. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. A small window was left untouched for future reference.

## Item 10
Polarised light microscopy of the ivory hair comb identified a drying oil bound with a little pine resin. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. Results were checked against laboratory reference material.

## Item 11
Polarised light microscopy of the leather bookbinding identified a double ground of chalk under lead white. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sampling was held to the minimum needed.

## Item 12
The marble portrait bust was rehoused in an aluminium honeycomb panel, padded with washed cotton jersey. Handling is now possible without touching the surface. The fit was adjusted twice before it was accepted. The mount maker was consulted before the design was fixed.
