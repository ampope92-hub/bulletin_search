Studio Bulletin, issue 90

## Item 1
Cleaning removed a discoloured natural resin coating from the oak chest with swabs of a hydrocarbon and alcohol blend, working in short overlapping passes. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 2
Raman microscopy of the glass roundel identified a drying oil bound with a little pine resin. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram.

## Item 3
A failed earlier repair in the porcelain dish was rejoined with a reversible animal glue. The join is visible on close inspection by design. Alignment was dry-fitted before any adhesive was introduced. Clamping pressure was kept deliberately light.

## Item 4
Cleaning removed a discoloured natural resin coating from the papier-mache tea tray with swabs of a hydrocarbon and alcohol blend, working from the least sensitive area outward. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water.

## Item 5
Cleaning removed sooty deposits from the silk altar frontal with a buffered enzyme solution, working with frequent renewal of the swab. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 6
Cleaning removed greasy handling grime from the oak chest with a rigid chelating gel, working in one direction only. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage.

## Item 7
Treatment of the crizzled surface on the porcelain dish used a microcrystalline wax, applied by nebuliser in several passes. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 8
Polarised light microscopy of the wool tapestry identified a drying oil bound with a little pine resin. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram.

## Item 9
The silver caddy spoon was rehoused in a barrier-film enclosure with an oxygen scavenger, padded with washed cotton jersey. Handling is now possible without touching the surface. The mount maker was consulted before the design was fixed. The fit was adjusted twice before it was accepted.

## Item 10
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the wool tapestry used a seaweed-derived size, applied with a fine brush under magnification. Testing preceded each change of material. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 11
Radiography of the vellum choir book identified a double ground of chalk under lead white. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram.

## Item 12
The porcelain dish is displayed at thirty-five per cent relative humidity under a two hundred lux limit with annual rotation. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter. The specification was agreed with the curator.
