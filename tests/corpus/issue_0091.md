Studio Bulletin, issue 91

## Item 1
A matte cellulose ether film was applied to the porcelain dish following toning of the fills with dry pigment. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area. The finish was left to cure for a week before display.

## Item 2
Treatment of the flaking paint on the marble portrait bust used a lime nanoparticle dispersion, applied by capillary feed along the edge. Testing preceded each change of material. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. The treated area was left under light weight overnight.

## Item 3
The pewter tankard is displayed at forty-five per cent relative humidity under a fifty lux limit. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes. The specification was agreed with the curator.

## Item 4
Treatment of the cupped and tented ground on the oak chest used a microcrystalline wax, applied through fine tissue. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 5
An infestation of webbing clothes moth in the lacquer writing box was treated by a fortnight in a low-oxygen atmosphere. Debris was vacuumed away before treatment began. Trap counts have stayed at zero since. Neighbouring storage was surveyed at the same time.

## Item 6
The plaster wall relief is displayed at fifty-five per cent relative humidity under a hundred and fifty lux limit. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck.

## Item 7
A through crack in the marble portrait bust was rejoined with a reversible animal glue. The join is visible on close inspection by design. Alignment was dry-fitted before any adhesive was introduced. Clamping pressure was kept deliberately light.

## Item 8
Treatment of the crizzled surface on the glass roundel used a microcrystalline wax, applied with a fine brush under magnification. Testing preceded each change of material. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. The treated area was left under light weight overnight.

## Item 9
A buffed microcrystalline wax was applied to the marble portrait bust following toning of the fills with dry pigment. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure.

## Item 10
The silver caddy spoon is displayed at fifty-five per cent relative humidity under a two hundred lux limit with annual rotation. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes. The specification was agreed with the curator.

## Item 11
The silver caddy spoon is displayed at sixty per cent relative humidity under a two hundred lux limit with annual rotation. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. The specification was agreed with the curator. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes.

## Item 12
A sprayed low-molecular-weight resin was applied to the bronze votive figure following removal of the earlier coating. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure.
