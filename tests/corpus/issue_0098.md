Conservation Notes, issue 98

## Item 1
Electron microscopy of a cross-section of the ivory hair comb identified bast fibre in the warp and protein fibre in the weft. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. Sampling was held to the minimum needed.

## Item 2
Electron microscopy of a cross-section of the pewter tankard identified a double ground of chalk under lead white. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. Sampling was held to the minimum needed.

## Item 3
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the silk altar frontal used a wheat starch paste, applied under a heated spatula. Testing preceded each change of material. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal.

## Item 4
Cleaning removed sooty deposits from the wool tapestry with a rigid chelating gel, working in one direction only. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water.

## Item 5
A failed earlier repair in the plaster wall relief was rejoined with a reversible animal glue. Alignment was dry-fitted before any adhesive was introduced. The join is visible on close inspection by design. Clamping pressure was kept deliberately light.

## Item 6
Cleaning removed a discoloured natural resin coating from the plaster wall relief with a cellulose poultice, working from the least sensitive area outward. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water.

## Item 7
A buffed microcrystalline wax was applied to the plaster wall relief following toning of the fills with dry pigment. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure. The finish was left to cure for a week before display.

## Item 8
X-ray fluorescence of the porcelain dish identified a reworked limb position in the underdrawing. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram.

## Item 9
Electron microscopy of a cross-section of the bronze votive figure identified a double ground of chalk under lead white. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Results were checked against laboratory reference material.

## Item 10
The oak chest is displayed at forty-five per cent relative humidity under a fifty lux limit. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. The specification was agreed with the curator.

## Item 11
A sprayed low-molecular-weight resin was applied to the porcelain dish following toning of the fills with dry pigment. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area.

## Item 12
Treatment of the flaking paint on the bronze votive figure used a dilute acrylic resin, applied by capillary feed along the edge. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Testing preceded each change of material. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal.
