Conservation Notes, issue 114

## Item 1
Cleaning removed hard calcareous crusts from the pewter tankard with swabs of a hydrocarbon and alcohol blend, working in one direction only. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. A small window was left untouched for future reference.

## Item 2
A brushed dammar varnish was applied to the lacquer writing box following consolidation of the powdering surface. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area.

## Item 3
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the linen sampler used a microcrystalline wax, applied with a fine brush under magnification. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Testing preceded each change of material.

## Item 4
Cleaning removed greasy handling grime from the papier-mache tea tray with grated vinyl crumb, working in short overlapping passes. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. A small window was left untouched for future reference.

## Item 5
A sprayed low-molecular-weight resin was applied to the oak chest following toning of the fills with dry pigment. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area.

## Item 6
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the linen sampler used an isinglass solution, applied with a fine brush under magnification. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Testing preceded each change of material. The treated area was left under light weight overnight.

## Item 7
X-ray fluorescence of the silk altar frontal identified an arsenic-bearing green in the border. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Sampling was held to the minimum needed.

## Item 8
Treatment of the cupped and tented ground on the plaster wall relief used a dilute acrylic resin, applied under a heated spatula. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. The treated area was left under light weight overnight.

## Item 9
Raman microscopy of the oak chest identified a mercury-based red beneath a later overpaint. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram.

## Item 10
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the vellum choir book used an isinglass solution, applied by nebuliser in several passes. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Testing preceded each change of material.

## Item 11
Raman microscopy of the vellum choir book identified bast fibre in the warp and protein fibre in the weft. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Results were checked against laboratory reference material.

## Item 12
Treatment of the flaking paint on the silver caddy spoon used a microcrystalline wax, applied through fine tissue. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Testing preceded each change of material. The treated area was left under light weight overnight.
