Collections Care Digest, issue 120

## Item 1
A detached handle in the oak chest was rejoined with hidden hardwood pegs. The repair can be taken apart again if required. The join is visible on close inspection by design. Clamping pressure was kept deliberately light.

## Item 2
The silk altar frontal is displayed at sixty per cent relative humidity under a hundred and fifty lux limit. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes.

## Item 3
Treatment of the blistered varnish on the papier-mache tea tray used a dilute acrylic resin, applied with a fine brush under magnification. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Testing preceded each change of material.

## Item 4
Treatment of the crizzled surface on the glass roundel used a cellulose ether gel, applied by capillary feed along the edge. Testing preceded each change of material. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. The treated area was left under light weight overnight.

## Item 5
The linen sampler was rehoused in a coated brass tension strap, padded with silk crepeline. Handling is now possible without touching the surface. The fit was adjusted twice before it was accepted. The mount maker was consulted before the design was fixed.

## Item 6
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the leather bookbinding used a cellulose ether gel, applied with a fine brush under magnification. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Testing preceded each change of material. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal.

## Item 7
The leather bookbinding is displayed at fifty-five per cent relative humidity under filtered daylight only. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes. The specification was agreed with the curator.

## Item 8
An infestation of carpet beetle larvae in the wool tapestry was treated by isolation and monitoring with blunder traps. No live activity was seen at the follow-up inspection. Neighbouring storage was surveyed at the same time. Trap counts have stayed at zero since.

## Item 9
Treatment of the cracked gilding on the oak chest used a cellulose ether gel, applied under a heated spatula. Testing preceded each change of material. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. The treated area was left under light weight overnight.

## Item 10
Treatment of the blistered varnish on the papier-mache tea tray used an isinglass solution, applied under a heated spatula. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Testing preceded each change of material.

## Item 11
The plaster wall relief is displayed at thirty-five per cent relative humidity under a hundred and fifty lux limit. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. The specification was agreed with the curator.

## Item 12
Cleaning removed a discoloured natural resin coating from the papier-mache tea tray with grated vinyl crumb, working in one direction only. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. A small window was left untouched for future reference.
