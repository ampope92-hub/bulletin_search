Collections Care Digest, issue 126

## Item 1
Cleaning removed greasy handling grime from the wool tapestry with a rigid chelating gel, working with frequent renewal of the swab. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage.

## Item 2
Treatment of the powdering pigment on the ivory hair comb used a wheat starch paste, applied under gentle vacuum suction. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Testing preceded each change of material. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal.

## Item 3
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the wool tapestry used a warm fish glue, applied by nebuliser in several passes. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Testing preceded each change of material.

## Item 4
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the silk altar frontal used a dilute acrylic resin, applied with a fine brush under magnification. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 5
The marble portrait bust is displayed at fifty-five per cent relative humidity under a two hundred lux limit with annual rotation. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes. The specification was agreed with the curator.

## Item 6
Infrared spectroscopy of the oak chest identified an arsenic-bearing green in the border. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram.

## Item 7
Treatment of the cupped and tented ground on the marble portrait bust used a microcrystalline wax, applied by nebuliser in several passes. Testing preceded each change of material. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. The treated area was left under light weight overnight.

## Item 8
The plaster wall relief was rehoused in a carved inert foam cradle, padded with unbuffered tissue. The housing carries a printed handling instruction. The mount maker was consulted before the design was fixed. The fit was adjusted twice before it was accepted.

## Item 9
Infrared spectroscopy of the oak chest identified bast fibre in the warp and protein fibre in the weft. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram.

## Item 10
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the leather bookbinding used a dilute acrylic resin, applied under a heated spatula. Testing preceded each change of material. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 11
Polarised light microscopy of the plaster wall relief identified bast fibre in the warp and protein fibre in the weft. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram.

## Item 12
An old split along the grain in the oak chest was rejoined with a shaped balsa fill. Clamping pressure was kept deliberately light. Alignment was dry-fitted before any adhesive was introduced. The join is visible on close inspection by design.
