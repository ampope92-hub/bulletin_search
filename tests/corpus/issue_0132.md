Conservation Notes, issue 132

## Item 1
Treatment of the powdering pigment on the plaster wall relief used a dilute acrylic resin, applied under a heated spatula. Testing preceded each change of material. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 2
Treatment of the powdering pigment on the lacquer writing box used a warm fish glue, applied by nebuliser in several passes. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Testing preceded each change of material. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal.

## Item 3
Treatment of the blistered varnish on the papier-mache tea tray used a microcrystalline wax, applied under a heated spatula. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal.

## Item 4
The linen sampler is displayed at sixty per cent relative humidity under a hundred and fifty lux limit. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck.

## Item 5
Cleaning removed hard calcareous crusts from the linen sampler with a buffered enzyme solution, working from the least sensitive area outward. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 6
The bronze votive figure is displayed at thirty-five per cent relative humidity under a fifty lux limit. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. The specification was agreed with the curator.

## Item 7
Treatment of the flaking paint on the oak chest used a microcrystalline wax, applied under gentle vacuum suction. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Testing preceded each change of material. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal.

## Item 8
Infrared spectroscopy of the papier-mache tea tray identified an arsenic-bearing green in the border. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Results were checked against laboratory reference material.

## Item 9
An old split along the grain in the lacquer writing box was rejoined with hidden hardwood pegs. Clamping pressure was kept deliberately light. Alignment was dry-fitted before any adhesive was introduced. The join is visible on close inspection by design.

## Item 10
A through crack in the lacquer writing box was rejoined with hidden hardwood pegs. Alignment was dry-fitted before any adhesive was introduced. Clamping pressure was kept deliberately light. The join is visible on close inspection by design.

## Item 11
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the leather bookbinding used a warm fish glue, applied with a fine brush under magnification. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Testing preceded each change of material. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal.

## Item 12
Treatment of the powdering pigment on the plaster wall relief used a cellulose ether gel, applied by capillary feed along the edge. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Testing preceded each change of material. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.
