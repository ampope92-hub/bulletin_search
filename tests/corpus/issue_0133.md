Conservation Notes, issue 133

## Item 1
Polarised light microscopy of the leather bookbinding identified a drying oil bound with a little pine resin. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sampling was held to the minimum needed.

## Item 2
The glass roundel is displayed at fifty-five per cent relative humidity under a fifty lux limit. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. The specification was agreed with the curator.

## Item 3
Raman microscopy of the linen sampler identified bast fibre in the warp and protein fibre in the weft. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram.

## Item 4
X-ray fluorescence of the ivory hair comb identified bast fibre in the warp and protein fibre in the weft. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sampling was held to the minimum needed.

## Item 5
Treatment of the flaking paint on the bronze votive figure used a dilute acrylic resin, applied under a heated spatula. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Testing preceded each change of material. The treated area was left under light weight overnight.

## Item 6
Cleaning removed hard calcareous crusts from the oak chest with swabs of a hydrocarbon and alcohol blend, working in short overlapping passes. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 7
The vellum choir book was rehoused in a carved inert foam cradle, padded with washed cotton jersey. The fit was adjusted twice before it was accepted. The mount maker was consulted before the design was fixed. The housing carries a printed handling instruction.

## Item 8
Treatment of the powdering pigment on the ivory hair comb used a wheat starch paste, applied under gentle vacuum suction. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Testing preceded each change of material. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal.

## Item 9
Cleaning removed adhesive residue from old tape from the lacquer writing box with a rigid chelating gel, working in one direction only. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage.

## Item 10
Electron microscopy of a cross-section of the vellum choir book identified a double ground of chalk under lead white. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. The finding is consistent with the attributed date.

## Item 11
A buffed microcrystalline wax was applied to the pewter tankard following consolidation of the powdering surface. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure.

## Item 12
Raman microscopy of the silver caddy spoon identified a double ground of chalk under lead white. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram.
