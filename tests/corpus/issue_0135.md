Studio Bulletin, issue 135

## Item 1
Electron microscopy of a cross-section of the silver caddy spoon identified a double ground of chalk under lead white. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sampling was held to the minimum needed.

## Item 2
Cleaning removed adhesive residue from old tape from the vellum choir book with swabs of a hydrocarbon and alcohol blend, working from the least sensitive area outward. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water.

## Item 3
The vellum choir book is displayed at fifty-five per cent relative humidity under filtered daylight only. The specification was agreed with the curator. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck.

## Item 4
The linen sampler is displayed at thirty-five per cent relative humidity under a two hundred lux limit with annual rotation. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. The specification was agreed with the curator. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter.

## Item 5
Treatment of the crizzled surface on the porcelain dish used a dilute acrylic resin, applied with a fine brush under magnification. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal.

## Item 6
A matte cellulose ether film was applied to the silk altar frontal following consolidation of the powdering surface. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area.

## Item 7
The plaster wall relief was rehoused in an aluminium honeycomb panel, padded with unbuffered tissue. The mount maker was consulted before the design was fixed. The fit was adjusted twice before it was accepted. The housing carries a printed handling instruction.

## Item 8
The linen sampler was rehoused in a carved inert foam cradle, padded with silk crepeline. Handling is now possible without touching the surface. The fit was adjusted twice before it was accepted. The housing carries a printed handling instruction.

## Item 9
Treatment of the powdering pigment on the marble portrait bust used a lime nanoparticle dispersion, applied by capillary feed along the edge. Testing preceded each change of material. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 10
Cleaning removed a discoloured natural resin coating from the pewter tankard with a rigid chelating gel, working in one direction only. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 11
The lacquer writing box is displayed at fifty-five per cent relative humidity under a hundred and fifty lux limit. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes. The specification was agreed with the curator.

## Item 12
Treatment of the delaminating veneer on the lacquer writing box used a dilute acrylic resin, applied under a heated spatula. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Testing preceded each change of material. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal.
