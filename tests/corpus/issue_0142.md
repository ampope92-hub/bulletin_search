Studio Bulletin, issue 142

## Item 1
A buffed microcrystalline wax was applied to the marble portrait bust following removal of the earlier coating. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure. The finish was left to cure for a week before display.

## Item 2
A through crack in the ivory hair comb was rejoined with a reversible animal glue. The repair can be taken apart again if required. The join is visible on close inspection by design. Alignment was dry-fitted before any adhesive was introduced.

## Item 3
Treatment of the powdering pigment on the oak chest used a microcrystalline wax, applied through fine tissue. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Testing preceded each change of material.

## Item 4
Treatment of the powdering pigment on the ivory hair comb used a warm fish glue, applied under a heated spatula. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Testing preceded each change of material.

## Item 5
Raman microscopy of the silver caddy spoon identified a mercury-based red beneath a later overpaint. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. The finding is consistent with the attributed date.

## Item 6
A buffed microcrystalline wax was applied to the pewter tankard following removal of the earlier coating. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area. The finish was left to cure for a week before display.

## Item 7
Treatment of the blistered varnish on the lacquer writing box used a microcrystalline wax, applied by nebuliser in several passes. Testing preceded each change of material. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 8
The wool tapestry is displayed at thirty-five per cent relative humidity under a two hundred lux limit with annual rotation. The specification was agreed with the curator. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter.

## Item 9
Treatment of the blistered varnish on the oak chest used a cellulose ether gel, applied under a heated spatula. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Testing preceded each change of material. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 10
The lacquer writing box is displayed at sixty per cent relative humidity under a fifty lux limit. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes. The specification was agreed with the curator. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck.

## Item 11
The papier-mache tea tray was rehoused in a carved inert foam cradle, padded with unbuffered tissue. Handling is now possible without touching the surface. The fit was adjusted twice before it was accepted. The mount maker was consulted before the design was fixed.

## Item 12
Infrared spectroscopy of the leather bookbinding identified bast fibre in the warp and protein fibre in the weft. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. Sampling was held to the minimum needed.
