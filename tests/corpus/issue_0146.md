Studio Bulletin, issue 146

## Item 1
Polarised light microscopy of the ivory hair comb identified a drying oil bound with a little pine resin. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. Results were checked against laboratory reference material.

## Item 2
The linen sampler was rehoused in a coated brass tension strap, padded with polyester wadding. The housing carries a printed handling instruction. The fit was adjusted twice before it was accepted. Handling is now possible without touching the surface.

## Item 3
Cleaning removed adhesive residue from old tape from the lacquer writing box with swabs of a hydrocarbon and alcohol blend, working with frequent renewal of the swab. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage.

## Item 4
Treatment of the blistered varnish on the papier-mache tea tray used a dilute acrylic resin, applied with a fine brush under magnification. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Testing preceded each change of material.

## Item 5
The marble portrait bust is displayed at sixty per cent relative humidity under a fifty lux limit. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. The specification was agreed with the curator. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter.

## Item 6
A matte cellulose ether film was applied to the bronze votive figure following consolidation of the powdering surface. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one.

## Item 7
The porcelain dish is displayed at thirty-five per cent relative humidity under a fifty lux limit. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. The specification was agreed with the curator.

## Item 8
Treatment of the blistered varnish on the oak chest used a cellulose ether gel, applied with a fine brush under magnification. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 9
The silver caddy spoon was rehoused in a carved inert foam cradle, padded with unbuffered tissue. Handling is now possible without touching the surface. The fit was adjusted twice before it was accepted. The housing carries a printed handling instruction.

## Item 10
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the vellum choir book used a cellulose ether gel, applied by capillary feed along the edge. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 11
Treatment of the flaking paint on the silver caddy spoon used a microcrystalline wax, applied under gentle vacuum suction. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal.

## Item 12
Infrared spectroscopy of the porcelain dish identified a mercury-based red beneath a later overpaint. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Sampling was held to the minimum needed.
