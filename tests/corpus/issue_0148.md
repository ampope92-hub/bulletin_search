Collections Care Digest, issue 148

## Item 1
The wool tapestry is displayed at thirty-five per cent relative humidity under a two hundred lux limit with annual rotation. The specification was agreed with the curator. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter.

## Item 2
A failed earlier repair in the silver caddy spoon was rejoined with a pin of matching alloy. Alignment was dry-fitted before any adhesive was introduced. Clamping pressure was kept deliberately light. The repair can be taken apart again if required.

## Item 3
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the linen sampler used a dilute acrylic resin, applied through fine tissue. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Testing preceded each change of material.

## Item 4
An old split along the grain in the oak chest was rejoined with hidden hardwood pegs. The join is visible on close inspection by design. The repair can be taken apart again if required. Clamping pressure was kept deliberately light.

## Item 5
Cleaning removed sooty deposits from the leather bookbinding with a rigid chelating gel, working with frequent renewal of the swab. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 6
A sprayed low-molecular-weight resin was applied to the linen sampler following toning of the fills with dry pigment. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure.

## Item 7
Radiography of the wool tapestry identified a double ground of chalk under lead white. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Results were checked against laboratory reference material.

## Item 8
The lacquer writing box is displayed at thirty-five per cent relative humidity under filtered daylight only. The specification was agreed with the curator. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes.

## Item 9
Cleaning removed a discoloured natural resin coating from the glass roundel with a cellulose poultice, working with frequent renewal of the swab. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. A small window was left untouched for future reference.

## Item 10
Cleaning removed a discoloured natural resin coating from the papier-mache tea tray with a rigid chelating gel, working in one direction only. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water.

## Item 11
Cleaning removed a discoloured natural resin coating from the papier-mache tea tray with grated vinyl crumb, working in one direction only. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. A small window was left untouched for future reference.

## Item 12
Infrared spectroscopy of the papier-mache tea tray identified a double ground of chalk under lead white. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram.
