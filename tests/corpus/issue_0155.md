Studio Bulletin, issue 155

## Item 1
Radiography of the marble portrait bust identified a drying oil bound with a little pine resin. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. The finding is consistent with the attributed date.

## Item 2
Cleaning removed insect frass from the papier-mache tea tray with a rigid chelating gel, working with frequent renewal of the swab. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 3
A detached handle in the lacquer writing box was rejoined with hidden hardwood pegs. The join is visible on close inspection by design. The repair can be taken apart again if required. Clamping pressure was kept deliberately light.

## Item 4
Cleaning removed sooty deposits from the bronze votive figure with a pulsed laser at low fluence, working from the least sensitive area outward. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage.

## Item 5
X-ray fluorescence of the marble portrait bust identified a mercury-based red beneath a later overpaint. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. Sampling was held to the minimum needed.

## Item 6
Infrared spectroscopy of the leather bookbinding identified a reworked limb position in the underdrawing. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Sampling was held to the minimum needed.

## Item 7
Polarised light microscopy of the silk altar frontal identified a reworked limb position in the underdrawing. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sampling was held to the minimum needed.

## Item 8
A through crack in the pewter tankard was rejoined with a pin of matching alloy. Alignment was dry-fitted before any adhesive was introduced. The join is visible on close inspection by design. The repair can be taken apart again if required.

## Item 9
Electron microscopy of a cross-section of the linen sampler identified a double ground of chalk under lead white. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Results were checked against laboratory reference material.

## Item 10
Cleaning removed sooty deposits from the wool tapestry with a buffered enzyme solution, working with frequent renewal of the swab. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage.

## Item 11
The plaster wall relief was rehoused in an aluminium honeycomb panel, padded with washed cotton jersey. The housing carries a printed handling instruction. Handling is now possible without touching the surface. The mount maker was consulted before the design was fixed.

## Item 12
The linen sampler was rehoused in a carved inert foam cradle, padded with washed cotton jersey. The fit was adjusted twice before it was accepted. The mount maker was consulted before the design was fixed. The housing carries a printed handling instruction.
