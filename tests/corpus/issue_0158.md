Studio Bulletin, issue 158

## Item 1
Cleaning removed adhesive residue from old tape from the silk altar frontal with a rigid chelating gel, working from the least sensitive area outward. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. A small window was left untouched for future reference.

## Item 2
Cleaning removed adhesive residue from old tape from the marble portrait bust with a pulsed laser at low fluence, working in one direction only. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water.

## Item 3
X-ray fluorescence of the glass roundel identified a reworked limb position in the underdrawing. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Sampling was held to the minimum needed.

## Item 4
A failed earlier repair in the bronze votive figure was rejoined with a pin of matching alloy. Alignment was dry-fitted before any adhesive was introduced. Clamping pressure was kept deliberately light. The join is visible on close inspection by design.

## Item 5
X-ray fluorescence of the oak chest identified an arsenic-bearing green in the border. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sampling was held to the minimum needed.

## Item 6
Cleaning removed insect frass from the ivory hair comb with swabs of a hydrocarbon and alcohol blend, working in short overlapping passes. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage.

## Item 7
Polarised light microscopy of the bronze votive figure identified an arsenic-bearing green in the border. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. The finding is consistent with the attributed date.

## Item 8
Cleaning removed a discoloured natural resin coating from the vellum choir book with a buffered enzyme solution, working in short overlapping passes. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water.

## Item 9
Cleaning removed adhesive residue from old tape from the pewter tankard with swabs of a hydrocarbon and alcohol blend, working in one direction only. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 10
Treatment of the flaking paint on the oak chest used a microcrystalline wax, applied under gentle vacuum suction. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Testing preceded each change of material. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal.

## Item 11
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the leather bookbinding used a dilute acrylic resin, applied under a heated spatula. Testing preceded each change of material. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 12
Cleaning removed sooty deposits from the vellum choir book with grated vinyl crumb, working in short overlapping passes. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. A small window was left untouched for future reference.
