Conservation Notes, issue 162

## Item 1
Polarised light microscopy of the oak chest identified a double ground of chalk under lead white. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram.

## Item 2
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the wool tapestry used a wheat starch paste, applied under a heated spatula. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Testing preceded each change of material. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 3
Cleaning removed adhesive residue from old tape from the wool tapestry with a buffered enzyme solution, working with frequent renewal of the swab. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 4
Treatment of the cracked gilding on the papier-mache tea tray used a dilute acrylic resin, applied through fine tissue. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal.

## Item 5
Cleaning removed sooty deposits from the wool tapestry with swabs of a hydrocarbon and alcohol blend, working with frequent renewal of the swab. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage.

## Item 6
Cleaning removed a discoloured natural resin coating from the leather bookbinding with grated vinyl crumb, working in short overlapping passes. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. A small window was left untouched for future reference.

## Item 7
Cleaning removed greasy handling grime from the bronze votive figure with swabs of a hydrocarbon and alcohol blend, working from the least sensitive area outward. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 8
Radiography of the wool tapestry identified bast fibre in the warp and protein fibre in the weft. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Results were checked against laboratory reference material.

## Item 9
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the wool tapestry used a cellulose ether gel, applied by capillary feed along the edge. Testing preceded each change of material. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. The treated area was left under light weight overnight.

## Item 10
A sprayed low-molecular-weight resin was applied to the oak chest following toning of the fills with dry pigment. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area.

## Item 11
An infestation of silverfish in the ivory hair comb was treated by a fortnight in a low-oxygen atmosphere. Neighbouring storage was surveyed at the same time. Debris was vacuumed away before treatment began. Trap counts have stayed at zero since.

## Item 12
A failed earlier repair in the oak chest was rejoined with hidden hardwood pegs. Alignment was dry-fitted before any adhesive was introduced. The repair can be taken apart again if required. Clamping pressure was kept deliberately light.
