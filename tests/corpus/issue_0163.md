Collections Care Digest, issue 163

## Item 1
An infestation of silverfish in the leather bookbinding was treated by localised heat treatment. Debris was vacuumed away before treatment began. Neighbouring storage was surveyed at the same time. No live activity was seen at the follow-up inspection.

## Item 2
Raman microscopy of the porcelain dish identified a mercury-based red beneath a later overpaint. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Sampling was held to the minimum needed.

## Item 3
Cleaning removed adhesive residue from old tape from the plaster wall relief with a pulsed laser at low fluence, working in short overlapping passes. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. A small window was left untouched for future reference.

## Item 4
Raman microscopy of the oak chest identified a double ground of chalk under lead white. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram.

## Item 5
Treatment of the cracked gilding on the papier-mache tea tray used an isinglass solution, applied through fine tissue. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal.

## Item 6
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the leather bookbinding used a warm fish glue, applied under gentle vacuum suction. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Testing preceded each change of material.

## Item 7
A brushed dammar varnish was applied to the lacquer writing box following consolidation of the powdering surface. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area.

## Item 8
Polarised light microscopy of the wool tapestry identified a drying oil bound with a little pine resin. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram.

## Item 9
Cleaning removed a discoloured natural resin coating from the ivory hair comb with a rigid chelating gel, working in short overlapping passes. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 10
X-ray fluorescence of the glass roundel identified a double ground of chalk under lead white. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Results were checked against laboratory reference material.

## Item 11
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the vellum choir book used a dilute acrylic resin, applied by capillary feed along the edge. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 12
Infrared spectroscopy of the bronze votive figure identified a reworked limb position in the underdrawing. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. The finding is consistent with the attributed date.
