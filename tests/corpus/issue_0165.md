Collections Care Digest, issue 165

## Item 1
Raman microscopy of the oak chest identified a double ground of chalk under lead white. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram.

## Item 2
An infestation of webbing clothes moth in the silk altar frontal was treated by controlled freezing with staged warming. No live activity was seen at the follow-up inspection. Debris was vacuumed away before treatment began. Trap counts have stayed at zero since.

## Item 3
Radiography of the porcelain dish identified a mercury-based red beneath a later overpaint. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. Sampling was held to the minimum needed.

## Item 4
Infrared spectroscopy of the lacquer writing box identified a reworked limb position in the underdrawing. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Results were checked against laboratory reference material.

## Item 5
A sprayed low-molecular-weight resin was applied to the silver caddy spoon following corrosion inhibition with an azole. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area.

## Item 6
A sprayed low-molecular-weight resin was applied to the leather bookbinding following consolidation of the powdering surface. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area.

## Item 7
Treatment of the flaking paint on the silver caddy spoon used a cellulose ether gel, applied under gentle vacuum suction. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. The treated area was left under light weight overnight.

## Item 8
The linen sampler was rehoused in a carved inert foam cradle, padded with silk crepeline. Handling is now possible without touching the surface. The fit was adjusted twice before it was accepted. The housing carries a printed handling instruction.

## Item 9
A sprayed low-molecular-weight resin was applied to the lacquer writing box following consolidation of the powdering surface. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure.

## Item 10
Treatment of the flaking paint on the silver caddy spoon used a microcrystalline wax, applied through fine tissue. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Testing preceded each change of material. The treated area was left under light weight overnight.

## Item 11
A buffed microcrystalline wax was applied to the oak chest following toning of the fills with dry pigment. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one. The finish was left to cure for a week before display.

## Item 12
The silk altar frontal was rehoused in a carved inert foam cradle, padded with silk crepeline. The housing carries a printed handling instruction. The mount maker was consulted before the design was fixed. Handling is now possible without touching the surface.
