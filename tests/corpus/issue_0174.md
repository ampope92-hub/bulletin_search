Conservation Notes, issue 174

## Item 1
Cleaning removed a discoloured natural resin coating from the bronze votive figure with a rigid chelating gel, working in one direction only. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 2
X-ray fluorescence of the bronze votive figure identified a double ground of chalk under lead white. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. The finding is consistent with the attributed date.

## Item 3
Cleaning removed hard calcareous crusts from the glass roundel with a cellulose poultice, working in short overlapping passes. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. A small window was left untouched for future reference.

## Item 4
Treatment of the powdering pigment on the marble portrait bust used a microcrystalline wax, applied by nebuliser in several passes. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Testing preceded each change of material.

## Item 5
A matte cellulose ether film was applied to the silver caddy spoon following removal of the earlier coating. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure.

## Item 6
An infestation of webbing clothes moth in the wool tapestry was treated by a fortnight in a low-oxygen atmosphere. Debris was vacuumed away before treatment began. Trap counts have stayed at zero since. No live activity was seen at the follow-up inspection.

## Item 7
Treatment of the blistered varnish on the papier-mache tea tray used an isinglass solution, applied under a heated spatula. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Testing preceded each change of material.

## Item 8
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the silk altar frontal used a wheat starch paste, applied with a fine brush under magnification. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Testing preceded each change of material. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal.

## Item 9
Infrared spectroscopy of the papier-mache tea tray identified an arsenic-bearing green in the border. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Results were checked against laboratory reference material.

## Item 10
X-ray fluorescence of the linen sampler identified a double ground of chalk under lead white. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram.

## Item 11
The silk altar frontal is displayed at fifty-five per cent relative humidity under a two hundred lux limit with annual rotation. The specification was agreed with the curator. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter.

## Item 12
Cleaning removed hard calcareous crusts from the silk altar frontal with a rigid chelating gel, working in short overlapping passes. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water.
