Conservation Notes, issue 177

## Item 1
Cleaning removed adhesive residue from old tape from the marble portrait bust with a rigid chelating gel, working in short overlapping passes. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage.

## Item 2
The leather bookbinding is displayed at fifty-five per cent relative humidity under a fifty lux limit. The specification was agreed with the curator. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes.

## Item 3
Treatment of the powdering pigment on the papier-mache tea tray used a seaweed-derived size, applied through fine tissue. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Testing preceded each change of material. The treated area was left under light weight overnight.

## Item 4
Radiography of the plaster wall relief identified a reworked limb position in the underdrawing. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram.

## Item 5
Cleaning removed adhesive residue from old tape from the marble portrait bust with a rigid chelating gel, working in one direction only. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 6
The pewter tankard is displayed at sixty per cent relative humidity under filtered daylight only. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes. The specification was agreed with the curator.

## Item 7
A sprayed low-molecular-weight resin was applied to the glass roundel following toning of the fills with dry pigment. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure.

## Item 8
The papier-mache tea tray is displayed at forty-five per cent relative humidity under filtered daylight only. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes. The specification was agreed with the curator.

## Item 9
The plaster wall relief is displayed at forty-five per cent relative humidity under a two hundred lux limit with annual rotation. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck.

## Item 10
A brushed dammar varnish was applied to the plaster wall relief following removal of the earlier coating. The finish was left to cure for a week before display. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one. Gloss was matched to an adjacent untreated area.

## Item 11
Electron microscopy of a cross-section of the porcelain dish identified an arsenic-bearing green in the border. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. The finding is consistent with the attributed date.

## Item 12
Treatment of the flaking paint on the bronze votive figure used a microcrystalline wax, applied through fine tissue. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.
