Conservation Notes, issue 185

## Item 1
Treatment of the cupped and tented ground on the plaster wall relief used a dilute acrylic resin, applied with a fine brush under magnification. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. The treated area was left under light weight overnight.

## Item 2
Treatment of the brittle fibres on the leather bookbinding used a cellulose ether gel, applied with a fine brush under magnification. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Testing preceded each change of material. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal.

## Item 3
X-ray fluorescence of the pewter tankard identified a drying oil bound with a little pine resin. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. Sampling was held to the minimum needed.

## Item 4
Infrared spectroscopy of the leather bookbinding identified a double ground of chalk under lead white. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Sampling was held to the minimum needed.

## Item 5
Cleaning removed adhesive residue from old tape from the marble portrait bust with a pulsed laser at low fluence, working in one direction only. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water.

## Item 6
An infestation of powderpost beetle in the lacquer writing box was treated by isolation and monitoring with blunder traps. Neighbouring storage was surveyed at the same time. No live activity was seen at the follow-up inspection. Trap counts have stayed at zero since.

## Item 7
The silver caddy spoon was rehoused in a barrier-film enclosure with an oxygen scavenger, padded with unbuffered tissue. The housing carries a printed handling instruction. The mount maker was consulted before the design was fixed. The fit was adjusted twice before it was accepted.

## Item 8
Cleaning removed adhesive residue from old tape from the leather bookbinding with a rigid chelating gel, working in short overlapping passes. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Solubility was tested at three separate points first. Clearance was checked under magnification at every stage.

## Item 9
Cleaning removed sooty deposits from the silk altar frontal with a buffered enzyme solution, working with frequent renewal of the swab. A small window was left untouched for future reference. Residues were rinsed away with deionised water. Solubility was tested at three separate points first.

## Item 10
Treatment of the powdering pigment on the ivory hair comb used a cellulose ether gel, applied with a fine brush under magnification. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Testing preceded each change of material. The treated area was left under light weight overnight.

## Item 11
The ivory hair comb is displayed at thirty-five per cent relative humidity under filtered daylight only. The specification was agreed with the curator. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck.

## Item 12
Treatment of the cracked gilding on the lacquer writing box used a dilute acrylic resin, applied by capillary feed along the edge. Testing preceded each change of material. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. The treated area was left under light weight overnight.
