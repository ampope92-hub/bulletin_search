Studio Bulletin, issue 192

## Item 1
Treatment of the cracked gilding on the papier-mache tea tray used an isinglass solution, applied under a heated spatula. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Testing preceded each change of material. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal.

## Item 2
A matte cellulose ether film was applied to the vellum choir book following consolidation of the powdering surface. Application took place in a filtered air enclosure. Two thin coats were preferred to one heavy one. The finish was left to cure for a week before display.

## Item 3
A shattered rim in the pewter tankard was rejoined with a reversible animal glue. The join is visible on close inspection by design. The repair can be taken apart again if required. Alignment was dry-fitted before any adhesive was introduced.

## Item 4
The lacquer writing box is displayed at forty-five per cent relative humidity under a two hundred lux limit with annual rotation. The specification was agreed with the curator. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes.

## Item 5
The leather bookbinding is displayed at fifty-five per cent relative humidity under a hundred and fifty lux limit. A buffer tray sits beneath the display deck. Conditions are reviewed whenever the gallery layout changes. Loggers in the case are downloaded each quarter.

## Item 6
Radiography of the plaster wall relief identified a reworked limb position in the underdrawing. Results were checked against laboratory reference material. The finding is consistent with the attributed date. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram.

## Item 7
Polarised light microscopy of the porcelain dish identified bast fibre in the warp and protein fibre in the weft. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. The finding is consistent with the attributed date.

## Item 8
Treatment of the powdering pigment on the ivory hair comb used a warm fish glue, applied under a heated spatula. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. Testing preceded each change of material.

## Item 9
Infrared spectroscopy of the glass roundel identified a mercury-based red beneath a later overpaint. Sample sites are marked on the accompanying diagram. Sampling was held to the minimum needed. The finding is consistent with the attributed date.

## Item 10
Treatment of the cracked gilding on the oak chest used a microcrystalline wax, applied by nebuliser in several passes. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. The treated area was left under light weight overnight.

## Item 11
Treatment of the blistered varnish on the papier-mache tea tray used a cellulose ether gel, applied with a fine brush under magnification. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down.

## Item 12
Treatment of the flaking paint on the oak chest used a microcrystalline wax, applied by capillary feed along the edge. The treated area was left under light weight overnight. Photographs record the surface before and after setting down. Work stopped once further gain would have been marginal.
